# MPM2D — curriculum alignment (checked 2026-10-18)

## Which curriculum applies

MPM2D (Principles of Mathematics, Grade 10, Academic) follows *The Ontario Curriculum, Grades 9
and 10: Mathematics* (2005), pages 46–52. It has three strands, plus the seven mathematical
process expectations, which are woven through every lesson:

- **QR — Quadratic Relations of the Form y = ax² + bx + c**: basic properties (data and curves of
  best fit, parabolas and constant second differences, key features, *comparing y = x² with y = 2ˣ
  and the meaning of zero and negative exponents*); transformations y = a(x − h)² + k; solving
  quadratic equations (expanding, factoring, zeros and factored form, real and non-real roots,
  completing the square without fractions, sketching from standard form, the quadratic formula);
  solving problems (zeros, max/min, realistic situations).
- **AG — Analytic Geometry**: linear systems (substitution, elimination, problems); midpoint, length,
  circle x² + y² = r², problems with slope/length/midpoint (right bisector, *distance from a point
  to a line*); verifying properties of triangles and quadrilaterals.
- **T — Trigonometry**: similar triangles (similarity vs congruence, realistic problems); right
  triangles (defining sin, cos, tan through similar triangles; solving; applications); acute
  triangles (exploring the development of the sine and cosine laws; solving; problems). The
  ambiguous case is not part of MPM2D.

All lesson, worksheet, bank, assignment and test content is original.

## Current materials (before the upgrade)

- Lessons: one long HTML block each (1.1 has three), no interactive explorers, no checked practice.
- Bank: 1000 questions (20/20/20 per topic for the chapters checked), **no written (long-answer)
  questions**, so paper chapter tests have no Part C.

- Assignments: one per lesson.

## Upgrade plan (same format as MTH1W)

1. Every chapter: lessons in the self-study format (5 worked examples · 5 checked practice
   questions · Q&A · interactive explorers and graphs) and a chapter review lesson.
2. One teacher worksheet PDF per lesson: notes with figures, 9 examples, 13 problems (3 / 5 / 5 by
   level), two-column question recap and answer key.
3. Hand-written bank: 20 easy / 20 medium / 20 hard per lesson, varied, hard = genuinely hard,
   figures where useful; plus 8–12 written questions per chapter for paper tests.
4. One assignment per lesson (Knowledge 3 · Thinking 2 · Communication 2 · Application 3).
5. Fill the gaps above: exponents (y = 2ˣ, zero and negative exponents) in chapter 4, distance from
   a point to a line in chapter 2, interval questions in 5.5.
