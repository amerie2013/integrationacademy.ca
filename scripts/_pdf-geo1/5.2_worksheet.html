5.2 Similarity Theorems (AA, SAS~, SSS~)
Similar triangles have the same shape but not necessarily the same size: corresponding angles are congruent, and corresponding sides are in a constant ratio (the scale factor). Three shortcuts prove similarity without checking every part. AA (Angle-Angle): two pairs of congruent angles are enough, since the third pair automatically matches (Angle-Sum Theorem). SAS~ (Side-Angle-Side): two pairs of sides in the same ratio, with the included angle congruent. SSS~ (Side-Side-Side): all three pairs of corresponding sides in the same ratio. A key skill is matching corresponding sides correctly — often this means sorting each triangle's sides from shortest to longest before comparing ratios. Once similarity is established, a proportion between corresponding sides (written in the order given by the similarity statement) solves for missing lengths, and perimeters of similar triangles share the same ratio as their sides.

Examples

Example 1: AA Similarity
∠A=50∘\angle A=50^{\circ}∠A=50∘, ∠B=70∘\angle B=70^{\circ}∠B=70∘ in △ABC\triangle ABC△ABC; ∠D=50∘\angle D=50^{\circ}∠D=50∘, ∠E=70∘\angle E=70^{\circ}∠E=70∘ in △DEF\triangle DEF△DEF. Determine if the triangles are similar, and write a similarity statement.
Solution
Matching angle pairs is enough to guarantee the same shape.
Compare the given angles: ∠A=∠D=50∘\angle A=\angle D=50^{\circ}∠A=∠D=50∘ and ∠B=∠E=70∘\angle B=\angle E=70^{\circ}∠B=∠E=70∘ — two pairs of congruent angles.
By the Angle-Sum Theorem, the third angles must also match: ∠C=∠F=180−50−70=60∘\angle C=\angle F=180-50-70=60^{\circ}∠C=∠F=180−50−70=60∘.
Two pairs of congruent angles is enough for similarity.
Answer: △ABC∼△DEF\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF△ABC∼△DEF by AA.

Example 7: Not similar (sorting reveals it)

Solution
Sort each set of sides: 3,4,53,4,53,4,5 and 6,8,126,8,126,8,12 (already in order).
Form the ratios: 63=2\dfrac{6}{3}=236​=2, 84=2\dfrac{8}{4}=248​=2, 125=2.4\dfrac{12}{5}=2.4512​=2.4.

Example 8: Third angle confirms AA
Two triangles each have angles 40∘40^{\circ}40∘ and 65∘65^{\circ}65∘. Find the third angle in each, and state whether the triangles are similar.
Solution
Apply the Angle-Sum Theorem to each triangle: third angle =180−40−65=75∘=180-40-65=75^{\circ}=180−40−65=75∘.
Both triangles have angle sets {40∘,65∘,75∘}\{40^{\circ}, 65^{\circ}, 75^{\circ}\}{40∘,65∘,75∘} — all three angles match.
Conclusion: the triangles are similar by AA (only two pairs needed to be checked; the third followed automatically).
