1.4 Pairs of Angles
Complementary, supplementary, vertical, and linear-pair angles are the building blocks behind almost every later angle proof, including parallel-line theorems in Chapter 2. The key upgrade here is recognizing that these relationships are theorems and postulates you can name and chain together — for instance, two angles vertical to the same angle must be congruent to each other, not just each congruent to the middle one. Expect algebra with the unknown inside the angle expressions, and at least one problem where you must find all four angles at an intersection from a single given value.

Examples

Example 1: Complementary angles
Two angles are complementary. One measures 35∘35^\circ35∘. Find the other.
Solution
Definition: complementary angles sum to 90∘90^\circ90∘ (they do not need to be adjacent).
Let the unknown angle be xxx: 35∘+x=90∘35^\circ + x = 90^\circ35∘+x=90∘.
Answer: x=55∘x = 55^\circx=55∘.
Example 2: Supplementary angles
Two angles are supplementary. One measures 110∘110^\circ110∘. Find the other.
Solution
Definition: supplementary angles sum to 180∘180^\circ180∘.
Let the unknown angle be xxx: 110∘+x=180∘110^\circ + x = 180^\circ110∘+x=180∘.
Answer: x=70∘x = 70^\circx=70∘.
Example 3: Vertical angles
Vertical angles measure 2x+102x + 102x+10 and 3x−53x - 53x−5. Find xxx.
Solution
∠1\angle 1∠1 and ∠3\angle 3∠3 are vertical, as are ∠2\angle 2∠2 and ∠4\angle 4∠4.
Vertical Angles Theorem: angles opposite each other at an intersection are congruent, so their measures are equal.
2x+10=3x−52x + 10 = 3x - 52x+10=3x−5
15=x15 = x15=x.
Check: both angles equal 2(15)+10=40∘2(15)+10 = 40^\circ2(15)+10=40∘. ✓
Example 4: Linear pair
Angles in a linear pair measure 4x4x4x and 2x+302x + 302x+30. Find xxx.
Solution
The two angles share a vertex and side, and their outer sides form a straight line.
Linear Pair Postulate: since the non-shared sides of the two angles form a straight line, the angles are supplementary.
4x+(2x+30)=1804x + (2x + 30) = 1804x+(2x+30)=180
6x+30=180⇒6x=150⇒x=256x + 30 = 180 \Rightarrow 6x = 150 \Rightarrow x = 256x+30=180⇒6x=150⇒x=25.
Check: 4(25)=100∘4(25)=100^\circ4(25)=100∘ and 2(25)+30=80∘2(25)+30=80^\circ2(25)+30=80∘; 100∘+80∘=180∘100^\circ+80^\circ=180^\circ100∘+80∘=180∘. ✓
Example 5: Adjacent vs. complementary
Are complementary angles always adjacent? Give a counterexample if not.
Solution
Definitions: adjacent angles must share a vertex and a side and must not overlap; complementary angles need only sum to 90∘90^\circ90∘ — no sharing is required.
Counterexample: a 30∘30^\circ30∘ angle in one corner of a room and a separate 60∘60^\circ60∘ angle across the room are complementary (30∘+60∘=90∘30^\circ+60^\circ=90^\circ30∘+60∘=90∘) but share no vertex or side.
Answer: No — complementary (and supplementary) angles are not required to be adjacent.
Example 6: Why vertical angles are congruent
If vertical angles measure xxx and 72∘72^\circ72∘, find xxx, and briefly explain why vertical angles are always congruent.
Solution
Proof idea: each of the two vertical angles forms a linear pair with the same adjacent angle, so each is supplementary to it.
By the Congruent Supplements Theorem, two angles supplementary to the same angle must be congruent to each other — so the vertical angles are congruent.
Answer: x=72∘x = 72^\circx=72∘.
Example 7: Complementary algebra
Complementary angles: 3x+53x + 53x+5 and 2x+152x + 152x+15. Find xxx.
Solution
Write the definition as an equation: the two measures sum to 90∘90^\circ90∘.
(3x+5)+(2x+15)=90(3x + 5) + (2x + 15) = 90(3x+5)+(2x+15)=90
5x+20=90⇒5x=70⇒x=145x + 20 = 90 \Rightarrow 5x = 70 \Rightarrow x = 145x+20=90⇒5x=70⇒x=14.
Check: 3(14)+5=47∘3(14)+5=47^\circ3(14)+5=47∘, 2(14)+15=43∘2(14)+15=43^\circ2(14)+15=43∘, sum =90∘=90^\circ=90∘. ✓
Example 8: Supplementary algebra
Supplementary angles: 5x5x5x and 4x+94x + 94x+9. Find both measures.
Solution
Write the definition as an equation: the two measures sum to 180∘180^\circ180∘.
5x+(4x+9)=180⇒9x+9=180⇒9x=171⇒x=195x + (4x + 9) = 180 \Rightarrow 9x + 9 = 180 \Rightarrow 9x = 171 \Rightarrow x = 195x+(4x+9)=180⇒9x+9=180⇒9x=171⇒x=19.
Substitute back: 5(19)=95∘5(19) = 95^\circ5(19)=95∘ and 4(19)+9=85∘4(19)+9 = 85^\circ4(19)+9=85∘.
Answer: 95∘95^\circ95∘ and 85∘85^\circ85∘ (check: 95+85=18095+85=18095+85=180). ✓
Example 9: Identify the pair
Two angles share a side and a vertex, and their non-shared sides form a straight line. What pair of angles are they, and what does that tell you about their measures?
Solution
Match the description to the definition: sharing a vertex and side, with non-shared sides forming a line, is exactly the definition of a linear pair.
By the Linear Pair Postulate, linear-pair angles are always supplementary.
Answer: A linear pair — the angles are adjacent and supplementary.
Example 10: All four angles from one intersection
Two lines intersect, forming four angles labeled 111, 222, 333, 444 in order around the intersection. If m∠1=55∘m\angle 1 = 55^\circm∠1=55∘, find the other three measures and state which pairs are vertical and which are linear pairs.
Solution
Angles 1 and 3 are vertical; angles 2 and 4 are vertical; consecutive angles form linear pairs.
∠1\angle 1∠1 and ∠3\angle 3∠3 are vertical (directly across the intersection), so by the Vertical Angles Theorem m∠3=55∘m\angle 3 = 55^\circm∠3=55∘.
∠1\angle 1∠1 and ∠2\angle 2∠2 are a linear pair (adjacent, sides forming a line), so they are supplementary: m∠2=180∘−55∘=125∘m\angle 2 = 180^\circ - 55^\circ = 125^\circm∠2=180∘−55∘=125∘.
∠4\angle 4∠4 is vertical to ∠2\angle 2∠2, so m∠4=125∘m\angle 4 = 125^\circm∠4=125∘.
Answer: m∠1=55∘m\angle1=55^\circm∠1=55∘, m∠2=125∘m\angle2=125^\circm∠2=125∘, m∠3=55∘m\angle3=55^\circm∠3=55∘, m∠4=125∘m\angle4=125^\circm∠4=125∘. Vertical pairs: (1,3)(1,3)(1,3), (2,4)(2,4)(2,4); linear pairs: (1,2),(2,3),(3,4),(4,1)(1,2),(2,3),(3,4),(4,1)(1,2),(2,3),(3,4),(4,1).
