8.3 Area of 2D Figures
Every area formula for a polygon can be built from the rectangle. A triangle is exactly half of the parallelogram formed by duplicating it and rotating the copy 180∘180^\circ180∘, so A=12bhA=\dfrac{1}{2}bhA=21​bh. A trapezoid can be doubled the same way into a parallelogram with base b1+b2b_1+b_2b1​+b2​, giving A=12(b1+b2)hA=\dfrac{1}{2}(b_1+b_2)hA=21​(b1​+b2​)h. A rhombus or kite splits along its diagonals into four right triangles that reassemble into a rectangle with sides d1d_1d1​ and d2d_2d2​, cut in half: A=12d1d2A=\dfrac{1}{2}d_1d_2A=21​d1​d2​. A regular polygon splits into nnn congruent triangles from the center, each with height equal to the apothem, so its total area is A=12⋅apothem⋅perimeterA=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\text{apothem}\cdot\text{perimeter}A=21​⋅apothem⋅perimeter. This worksheet also uses composite-figure decomposition and Heron's formula, A=s(s−a)(s−b)(s−c)A=\sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}A=s(s−a)(s−b)(s−c)​ with s=a+b+c2s=\dfrac{a+b+c}{2}s=2a+b+c​, for triangles where only the three side lengths are known.

Examples

Example 1: Triangle area
A triangle has base 101010 and height 666. Find its area.
Solution
Height is measured perpendicular to the chosen base.
Identify the base and height: b=10b=10b=10, h=6h=6h=6.
Apply the triangle area formula:
A=12bh=12(10)(6)=30A=\dfrac{1}{2}bh=\dfrac{1}{2}(10)(6)=30A=21​bh=21​(10)(6)=30.
Answer: 303030.
Example 2: Parallelogram area
A parallelogram has base 121212 and height 555. Find its area.
Solution
Area === base ×\times× height, just like a rectangle sheared sideways.
Identify the base and height: b=12b=12b=12, h=5h=5h=5.
Apply the parallelogram area formula:
A=bh=12(5)=60A=bh=12(5)=60A=bh=12(5)=60.
Answer: 606060.
Example 3: Trapezoid area (and why we average the bases)
A trapezoid has bases 888 and 141414 and height 555. Find its area.
Solution
Two copies of the trapezoid form a parallelogram with base b1+b2b_1+b_2b1​+b2​.
Two congruent copies of the trapezoid fit together to form a parallelogram with base 8+14=228+14=228+14=22 and height 555, area =22(5)=110=22(5)=110=22(5)=110.
The trapezoid is half of that parallelogram: A=12(8+14)(5)=12(22)(5)=55A=\dfrac{1}{2}(8+14)(5)=\dfrac{1}{2}(22)(5)=55A=21​(8+14)(5)=21​(22)(5)=55.
Answer: 555555.
Example 4: Rhombus area from diagonals
A rhombus has diagonals 101010 and 161616. Find its area.
Solution
The diagonals split the rhombus into four right triangles that rearrange into a d1d_1d1​-by-d2d_2d2​ rectangle, cut in half.
Identify the diagonals: d1=10d_1=10d1​=10, d2=16d_2=16d2​=16.
Apply the rhombus area formula:
A=12d1d2=12(10)(16)=80A=\dfrac{1}{2}d_1d_2=\dfrac{1}{2}(10)(16)=80A=21​d1​d2​=21​(10)(16)=80.
Answer: 808080.
Example 5: Regular hexagon area (two ways)
A regular hexagon has side length 444. Find its area.
Solution
A regular hexagon splits into 666 congruent equilateral triangles.
Method 1 (six equilateral triangles): each has area 34s2=34(16)=43\dfrac{\sqrt3}{4}s^2=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{4}(16)=4\sqrt343​​s2=43​​(16)=43​; total =6(43)=243=6(4\sqrt3)=24\sqrt3=6(43​)=243​.
Method 2 (formula): A=332s2=332(16)=243A=\dfrac{3\sqrt3}{2}s^2=\dfrac{3\sqrt3}{2}(16)=24\sqrt3A=233​​s2=233​​(16)=243​.
Both agree.
Answer: 24324\sqrt3243​.
Example 6: Rectangle area
A rectangle has length 999 and width 444. Find its area.
Solution
Identify the length and width: l=9l=9l=9, w=4w=4w=4.
Apply the rectangle area formula:
A=lw=9(4)=36A=lw=9(4)=36A=lw=9(4)=36.
Answer: 363636.
Example 7: Composite figure by decomposition
An L-shaped room is a 10×810\times810×8 rectangle with a 3×43\times43×4 rectangular corner removed. Find the room's area.
Solution
Area of the full rectangle: 10×8=8010\times8=8010×8=80.
Area of the removed corner: 3×4=123\times4=123×4=12.
Area of the L-shaped room: 80−12=6880-12=6880−12=68.
Answer: 686868 square units.
Example 8: Regular polygon from apothem and perimeter
A regular polygon has apothem 444 and perimeter 303030. Find its area.
Solution
The polygon splits into nnn congruent triangles from the center, each with height === apothem and total base length === perimeter, so A=12⋅apothem⋅perimeterA=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\text{apothem}\cdot\text{perimeter}A=21​⋅apothem⋅perimeter:
A=12(4)(30)=60A=\dfrac{1}{2}(4)(30)=60A=21​(4)(30)=60.
Answer: 606060.
Example 9: Heron's formula — area from three sides
A triangle has sides 131313, 141414, and 151515. Find its area using Heron's formula.
Solution
Semi-perimeter: s=13+14+152=422=21s=\dfrac{13+14+15}{2}=\dfrac{42}{2}=21s=213+14+15​=242​=21.
A=s(s−a)(s−b)(s−c)=21(21−13)(21−14)(21−15)=21⋅8⋅7⋅6A=\sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}=\sqrt{21(21-13)(21-14)(21-15)}=\sqrt{21\cdot8\cdot7\cdot6}A=s(s−a)(s−b)(s−c)​=21(21−13)(21−14)(21−15)​=21⋅8⋅7⋅6​.
21⋅8=16821\cdot8=16821⋅8=168; 168⋅7=1176168\cdot7=1176168⋅7=1176; 1176⋅6=70561176\cdot6=70561176⋅6=7056.
A=7056=84A=\sqrt{7056}=84A=7056​=84.
Answer: 848484.
Example 10: Algebra with a kite's diagonals
A kite's diagonals are 3x3x3x and 4x4x4x, and its area is 545454. Find xxx and both diagonal lengths.
Solution
A=12d1d2=12(3x)(4x)=6x2A=\dfrac{1}{2}d_1d_2=\dfrac{1}{2}(3x)(4x)=6x^2A=21​d1​d2​=21​(3x)(4x)=6x2.
6x2=54⇒x2=9⇒x=36x^2=54 \Rightarrow x^2=9 \Rightarrow x=36x2=54⇒x2=9⇒x=3.
Diagonals: 3x=93x=93x=9 and 4x=124x=124x=12.
Check: 12(9)(12)=54\dfrac{1}{2}(9)(12)=5421​(9)(12)=54. ✓
