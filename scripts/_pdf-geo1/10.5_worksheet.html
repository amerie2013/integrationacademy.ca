10.5 Solids of Revolution
Spinning a flat 2D region a full 360∘360^\circ360∘ around a line (the axis of revolution) sweeps out a 3D solid of revolution. The three building blocks are: a rectangle about one of its sides sweeps a cylinder; a right triangle about a leg sweeps a cone; and a semicircle about its diameter sweeps a sphere. More advanced regions build more advanced solids: a right trapezoid about its perpendicular leg sweeps a frustum (a cone with the tip cut off), a rectangle that does not touch the axis sweeps a hollow cylinder (tube) — found by subtracting an inner cylinder from an outer one — and a region offset entirely from the axis sweeps a torus (donut shape). This worksheet identifies the solid and computes its volume.

Examples

Example 1: Square about a side
A 5×55\times55×5 square is rotated 360∘360^\circ360∘ about one of its sides. Identify the solid and find its volume.
Solution
A rectangle (square is a special rectangle) about a side sweeps a cylinder.
The side of rotation becomes the axis; the opposite side sweeps out a circle of radius 555. Since both dimensions are 555, this is a cylinder with r=5r=5r=5, h=5h=5h=5.
V=πr2h=π(25)(5)=125πV=\pi r^{2}h=\pi(25)(5)=125\piV=πr2h=π(25)(5)=125π.
Answer: cylinder; V=125πV=125\piV=125π.
Example 2: Rectangle about a side
A 4×64\times64×6 rectangle is rotated 360∘360^\circ360∘ about its side of length 666. Identify the solid and find its volume.
Solution
The side of length 666 is the axis (becomes the height); the perpendicular side of length 444 sweeps out the radius. Cylinder with r=4r=4r=4, h=6h=6h=6.
V=πr2h=π(16)(6)=96πV=\pi r^{2}h=\pi(16)(6)=96\piV=πr2h=π(16)(6)=96π.
Answer: cylinder; V=96πV=96\piV=96π.
Example 3: Right triangle about a leg
A right triangle with legs 333 and 444 is rotated about the leg of length 444. Identify the solid and find its volume.
Solution
A right triangle about a leg sweeps a cone: the other leg becomes the radius, the axis leg becomes the height.
The leg of length 444 is the axis (height); the leg of length 333 sweeps out the radius. Cone with r=3r=3r=3, h=4h=4h=4.
V=13πr2h=13π(9)(4)=36π3=12πV=\dfrac13\pi r^{2}h=\dfrac13\pi(9)(4)=\dfrac{36\pi}{3}=12\piV=31​πr2h=31​π(9)(4)=336π​=12π.
Answer: cone; V=12πV=12\piV=12π.
Example 4: Semicircle about its diameter
A semicircle of radius 555 is rotated about its diameter. Identify the solid and find its volume.
Solution
A semicircle about its diameter sweeps a full sphere.
Every point on the semicircle is a distance 555 from the center of the diameter, so the swept solid is a sphere of radius 555.
V=43πr3=43π(125)=500π3V=\dfrac43\pi r^{3}=\dfrac43\pi(125)=\dfrac{500\pi}{3}V=34​πr3=34​π(125)=3500π​.
Answer: sphere; V=500π3V=\dfrac{500\pi}{3}V=3500π​.
Example 5: Same rectangle, different axis
The same 4×64\times64×6 rectangle from Example 2 is instead rotated about its side of length 444. Find the new volume.
Solution
Now the side of length 444 is the axis (height), and the side of length 666 sweeps the radius — the roles of rrr and hhh swap. Cylinder with r=6r=6r=6, h=4h=4h=4.
V=πr2h=π(36)(4)=144πV=\pi r^{2}h=\pi(36)(4)=144\piV=πr2h=π(36)(4)=144π.
Answer: cylinder; V=144πV=144\piV=144π. Notice this is a different volume from Example 2 (96π96\pi96π) — the choice of axis matters!
Example 6: Right trapezoid about its perpendicular leg — a frustum
A right trapezoid has parallel sides 666 and 222 and a perpendicular leg (height) of 999. It is rotated about that perpendicular leg. Identify the solid and find its volume.
Solution
A right trapezoid's two parallel sides become the two radii of a frustum when rotated about the perpendicular leg.
The perpendicular leg is the axis; the two parallel sides sweep out two different circles — a frustum (cone with the tip sliced off) with radii R=6R=6R=6, r=2r=2r=2, and height h=9h=9h=9.
Frustum volume: V=13πh(R2+Rr+r2)=13π(9)(36+12+4)=13π(9)(52)V=\dfrac13\pi h(R^{2}+Rr+r^{2})=\dfrac13\pi(9)(36+12+4)=\dfrac13\pi(9)(52)V=31​πh(R2+Rr+r2)=31​π(9)(36+12+4)=31​π(9)(52)
V=3π(52)=156πV=3\pi(52)=156\piV=3π(52)=156π.
Answer: frustum; V=156πV=156\piV=156π.
Example 7: Rectangle offset from the axis — a hollow cylinder
A rectangle spans from 333 to 555 units away from the axis (so it does not touch the axis) and is 101010 units tall. It is rotated 360∘360^\circ360∘. Identify the solid and find its volume.
Solution
Since the rectangle never touches the axis, it sweeps out a hollow cylinder (tube): an outer cylinder of radius R=5R=5R=5 minus an inner cylinder of radius r=3r=3r=3, both height 101010.
V=πR2h−πr2h=πh(R2−r2)=π(10)(25−9)=π(10)(16)V=\pi R^{2}h-\pi r^{2}h=\pi h(R^{2}-r^{2})=\pi(10)(25-9)=\pi(10)(16)V=πR2h−πr2h=πh(R2−r2)=π(10)(25−9)=π(10)(16)
V=160πV=160\piV=160π.
Answer: hollow cylinder; V=160πV=160\piV=160π.
Example 8: Composite region — cylinder + cone
A rectangle (4×64\times64×6, axis along the side of length 666) sits directly below a right triangle (legs 444 and 555, axis along the leg of length 555), sharing the same radius 444 and the same axis. Both are rotated together. Find the total volume of the resulting solid.
Solution
Stacking a rectangle and a right triangle on the same axis, then revolving, produces a cylinder topped with a cone.
Cylinder part: r=4r=4r=4, h=6h=6h=6: V1=π(16)(6)=96πV_1=\pi(16)(6)=96\piV1​=π(16)(6)=96π.
Cone part: r=4r=4r=4, h=5h=5h=5: V2=13π(16)(5)=80π3V_2=\dfrac13\pi(16)(5)=\dfrac{80\pi}{3}V2​=31​π(16)(5)=380π​.
Total: V=96π+80π3=288π3+80π3=368π3V=96\pi+\dfrac{80\pi}{3}=\dfrac{288\pi}{3}+\dfrac{80\pi}{3}=\dfrac{368\pi}{3}V=96π+380π​=3288π​+380π​=3368π​.
Answer: V=368π3V=\dfrac{368\pi}{3}V=3368π​.
Example 9: Right triangle about its hypotenuse — a bicone
A right triangle with legs 666 and 888 (hypotenuse 101010) is rotated about its hypotenuse. Identify the solid and find its volume.
Solution
Rotating about the hypotenuse (instead of a leg) sweeps out two cones joined base-to-base — a bicone — because the altitude from the right angle to the hypotenuse becomes the shared radius, and the two segments of the hypotenuse become the two heights.
Shared radius (altitude to the hypotenuse): r=(leg1)(leg2)hyp=6×810=4.8r=\dfrac{(\text{leg}_1)(\text{leg}_2)}{\text{hyp}}=\dfrac{6\times8}{10}=4.8r=hyp(leg1​)(leg2​)​=106×8​=4.8.
Heights (the two segments the altitude divides the hypotenuse into): h1+h2=10h_1+h_2=10h1​+h2​=10 (they sum to the full hypotenuse — no need to find each one separately since the volume formula only needs the total).
V=13πr2(h1+h2)=13π(4.8)2(10)=13π(23.04)(10)=76.8π=384π5V=\dfrac13\pi r^{2}(h_1+h_2)=\dfrac13\pi(4.8)^{2}(10)=\dfrac13\pi(23.04)(10)=76.8\pi=\dfrac{384\pi}{5}V=31​πr2(h1​+h2​)=31​π(4.8)2(10)=31​π(23.04)(10)=76.8π=5384π​.
Answer: bicone; V=384π5=76.8πV=\dfrac{384\pi}{5}=76.8\piV=5384π​=76.8π.
Example 10: Region offset entirely from the axis — a torus
A semicircle of radius 333 is centered 101010 units from the axis of revolution (the semicircle never touches the axis). It is rotated 360∘360^\circ360∘. Identify the solid and find its volume.
Solution
Because the region is fully offset from the axis, this sweeps out a torus (donut shape), not a sphere. Its volume formula is V=2π2Rr2V=2\pi^{2}Rr^{2}V=2π2Rr2, where RRR is the distance from the axis to the tube's center and rrr is the tube's (circle's) radius.
V=2π2(10)(3)2=2π2(10)(9)=180π2V=2\pi^{2}(10)(3)^{2}=2\pi^{2}(10)(9)=180\pi^{2}V=2π2(10)(3)2=2π2(10)(9)=180π2.
Answer: torus; V=180π2V=180\pi^{2}V=180π2.
