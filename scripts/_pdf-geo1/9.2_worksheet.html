9.2 Inscribed Angles
An inscribed angle has its vertex on the circle and its two sides are chords. The Inscribed Angle Theorem says an inscribed angle is always half its intercepted arc: m∠=12marc^m\angle=\dfrac{1}{2}m\widehat{\text{arc}}m∠=21​marc. Two immediate consequences: inscribed angles that intercept the same arc are congruent to each other, and an inscribed angle is half of the central angle on the same arc (since the central angle equals the arc). A special case called Thales' Theorem follows directly: if a triangle is inscribed in a circle with one side as a diameter, the angle opposite that diameter is always a right angle, because a diameter's arc is 180∘180^\circ180∘ and half of 180∘180^\circ180∘ is 90∘90^\circ90∘. This worksheet includes the reasoning behind the theorem, not just its use.

Examples

Example 1: Angle from an arc
An inscribed angle intercepts an arc of 84∘84^\circ84∘. Find the angle.
Solution
Inscribed angle CCC is half of arc ABABAB.
Identify the theorem: an inscribed angle is always half of its intercepted arc, m∠=12marc^m\angle=\dfrac{1}{2}m\widehat{\text{arc}}m∠=21​marc.
Compute: m∠=12(84∘)=42∘m\angle=\dfrac{1}{2}(84^\circ)=42^\circm∠=21​(84∘)=42∘.
Answer: 42∘42^\circ42∘.
Example 2: Arc from an angle
An inscribed angle measures 55∘55^\circ55∘. Find its intercepted arc.
Solution
Identify: this reverses the Inscribed Angle Theorem — since the angle is half the arc, the arc must be double the angle.
Compute: arc =2×55∘=110∘=2\times55^\circ=110^\circ=2×55∘=110∘.
Answer: 110∘110^\circ110∘.
Example 3: Angles on the same arc are congruent
Inscribed angles ∠ACB\angle ACB∠ACB and ∠ADB\angle ADB∠ADB both intercept arc ABABAB: m∠ACB=(3x+5)∘m\angle ACB=(3x+5)^\circm∠ACB=(3x+5)∘ and m∠ADB=(4x−10)∘m\angle ADB=(4x-10)^\circm∠ADB=(4x−10)∘. Find xxx and the angle measure.
Solution
Angles intercepting the same arc are congruent:
3x+5=4x−103x+5=4x-103x+5=4x−10
x=15x=15x=15.
Angle =3(15)+5=50∘=3(15)+5=50^\circ=3(15)+5=50∘.
Answer: x=15x=15x=15; each angle =50∘=50^\circ=50∘.
Example 4: Thales' Theorem
Triangle ABCABCABC is inscribed in a circle with diameter AB‾\overline{AB}AB. m∠A=35∘m\angle A=35^\circm∠A=35∘. Find m∠Bm\angle Bm∠B.
Solution
A triangle inscribed with a diameter as one side always has a right angle opposite that diameter.
Since AB‾\overline{AB}AB is a diameter, its arc is a semicircle (180∘180^\circ180∘), so the inscribed angle at CCC is 12(180∘)=90∘\dfrac{1}{2}(180^\circ)=90^\circ21​(180∘)=90∘.
The triangle's angles sum to 180∘180^\circ180∘: m∠B=180∘−90∘−35∘=55∘m\angle B=180^\circ-90^\circ-35^\circ=55^\circm∠B=180∘−90∘−35∘=55∘.
Answer: 55∘55^\circ55∘.
Example 5: Central angle vs. inscribed angle
Central angle ∠AOB=100∘\angle AOB=100^\circ∠AOB=100∘. Point CCC lies on the major arc. Find inscribed angle ∠ACB\angle ACB∠ACB.
Solution
The central angle equals its intercepted arc: mAB^=100∘m\widehat{AB}=100^\circmAB=100∘.
The inscribed angle on that same arc is half: m∠ACB=12(100∘)=50∘m\angle ACB=\dfrac{1}{2}(100^\circ)=50^\circm∠ACB=21​(100∘)=50∘.
Answer: 50∘50^\circ50∘.
Example 6: Angle from an arc
An inscribed angle intercepts an arc of 96∘96^\circ96∘. Find the angle.
Solution
Identify: apply the Inscribed Angle Theorem directly — the angle is half the arc.
Compute: 12(96∘)=48∘\dfrac{1}{2}(96^\circ)=48^\circ21​(96∘)=48∘.
Answer: 48∘48^\circ48∘.
Example 7: Arc from an angle
An inscribed angle measures 63∘63^\circ63∘. Find its intercepted arc.
Solution
Identify: work backward from the angle to the arc by doubling.
Compute: 2(63∘)=126∘2(63^\circ)=126^\circ2(63∘)=126∘.
Answer: 126∘126^\circ126∘.
Example 8: Same-arc equation
Two inscribed angles on the same arc measure (2x+8)∘(2x+8)^\circ(2x+8)∘ and (5x−19)∘(5x-19)^\circ(5x−19)∘. Find xxx and the angle.
Solution
2x+8=5x−192x+8=5x-192x+8=5x−19
27=3x⇒x=927=3x \Rightarrow x=927=3x⇒x=9.
Angle =2(9)+8=26∘=2(9)+8=26^\circ=2(9)+8=26∘.
Answer: x=9x=9x=9; angle =26∘=26^\circ=26∘.
Example 9: Semicircle triangle
A right triangle is inscribed with its hypotenuse as a diameter. One acute angle is 28∘28^\circ28∘. Find the other.
Solution
Identify: by Thales' Theorem, the angle opposite a diameter is always 90∘90^\circ90∘, so this is a right triangle with one leg-angle already known.
Compute the missing angle using the triangle angle sum: 180∘−90∘−28∘=62∘180^\circ-90^\circ-28^\circ=62^\circ180∘−90∘−28∘=62∘.
Answer: 62∘62^\circ62∘.
Example 10: Why is the inscribed angle half the arc?
Prove the Inscribed Angle Theorem for the case where one side of the inscribed angle is a diameter: circle with center OOO, diameter CB‾\overline{CB}CB, and point AAA on the circle, forming inscribed angle ∠ACB\angle ACB∠ACB.
Solution
Draw radius OA‾\overline{OA}OA. Since OA=OC=rOA=OC=rOA=OC=r, triangle OACOACOAC is isosceles, so its base angles are equal: m∠OCA=m∠OAC=xm\angle OCA=m\angle OAC=xm∠OCA=m∠OAC=x (note ∠OCA\angle OCA∠OCA is the same angle as ∠ACB\angle ACB∠ACB).
Because CCC, OOO, BBB lie on a straight diameter, ∠AOB\angle AOB∠AOB is the exterior angle of triangle OACOACOAC at vertex OOO.
By the Exterior Angle Theorem, an exterior angle equals the sum of the two remote interior angles: m∠AOB=m∠OCA+m∠OAC=x+x=2xm\angle AOB=m\angle OCA+m\angle OAC=x+x=2xm∠AOB=m∠OCA+m∠OAC=x+x=2x.
But ∠AOB\angle AOB∠AOB is a central angle, so m∠AOB=mAB^m\angle AOB=m\widehat{AB}m∠AOB=mAB. Therefore mAB^=2xm\widehat{AB}=2xmAB=2x, which means x=12mAB^x=\dfrac{1}{2}m\widehat{AB}x=21​mAB — exactly the Inscribed Angle Theorem.
Example 11: Application — lighthouses on a bay
A curved shoreline is modeled as an arc of a circle. Two lighthouses sit on the shoreline, and a ship at a third point on the same circle sights both lighthouses. The arc between the lighthouses that does not contain the ship measures 130∘130^\circ130∘. Find the angle at which the ship views the two lighthouses.
Solution
The ship's viewing angle is an inscribed angle intercepting the arc between the lighthouses that does not contain the ship:
m∠=12(130∘)=65∘m\angle=\dfrac{1}{2}(130^\circ)=65^\circm∠=21​(130∘)=65∘.
Answer: 65∘65^\circ65∘.
