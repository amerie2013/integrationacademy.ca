12.1 Probability and Sample Spaces
The sample space SSS is the set of all possible outcomes of an experiment; an event EEE is any subset of those outcomes. For equally likely outcomes, P(E)=n(E)n(S)P(E)=\dfrac{n(E)}{n(S)}P(E)=n(S)n(E)​ — the count of favorable outcomes over the total count. Two techniques make counting outcomes manageable: listing/organizing the full sample space (a grid for two dice, a tree for sequential flips), and the complement rule P(E′)=1−P(E)P(E')=1-P(E)P(E′)=1−P(E), which is often far faster than counting the event directly — especially for "at least one" questions. All probability answers in this course should be exact fractions in lowest terms.

Examples

Example 1: Single die
A fair six-sided die is rolled. Find P(rolling a 4)P(\text{rolling a }4)P(rolling a 4).
Solution
Sample space: S={1,2,3,4,5,6}S=\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}S={1,2,3,4,5,6}, so n(S)=6n(S)=6n(S)=6. Only one outcome is favorable.
P(4)=16P(4)=\dfrac{1}{6}P(4)=61​.
Answer: 16\dfrac{1}{6}61​.
Example 2: Event with multiple outcomes
A fair die is rolled. Find P(even)P(\text{even})P(even).
Solution
Favorable outcomes {2,4,6} highlighted out of the full sample space {1,...,6}.
Favorable outcomes: {2,4,6}\{2,4,6\}{2,4,6}, so n(E)=3n(E)=3n(E)=3; n(S)=6n(S)=6n(S)=6.
P(even)=36=12P(\text{even})=\dfrac{3}{6}=\dfrac{1}{2}P(even)=63​=21​ (always reduce to lowest terms).
Answer: 12\dfrac{1}{2}21​.
Example 3: Marbles
A bag has 555 red, 333 blue, and 222 green marbles. One is drawn at random. Find P(green)P(\text{green})P(green).
Solution
n(S)=5+3+2=10n(S)=5+3+2=10n(S)=5+3+2=10 total marbles; n(E)=2n(E)=2n(E)=2 green.
P(green)=210=15P(\text{green})=\dfrac{2}{10}=\dfrac{1}{5}P(green)=102​=51​.
Answer: 15\dfrac{1}{5}51​.
Example 4: Complement rule
Same bag (555 red, 333 blue, 222 green). Find P(not red)P(\text{not red})P(not red) using the complement.
Solution
P(red)=510=12P(\text{red})=\dfrac{5}{10}=\dfrac12P(red)=105​=21​.
P(not red)=1−P(red)=1−12=12P(\text{not red})=1-P(\text{red})=1-\dfrac12=\dfrac12P(not red)=1−P(red)=1−21​=21​.
Check directly: not red means blue or green: 3+210=510=12\dfrac{3+2}{10}=\dfrac{5}{10}=\dfrac12103+2​=105​=21​. ✓
Answer: 12\dfrac1221​.
Example 5: Two coins — sample space tree
Two fair coins are flipped. Find P(exactly one head)P(\text{exactly one head})P(exactly one head).
Solution
The tree shows all 4 equally likely outcomes: HH, HT, TH, TT.
S={HH,HT,TH,TT}S=\{HH,HT,TH,TT\}S={HH,HT,TH,TT}, so n(S)=4n(S)=4n(S)=4. "Exactly one head" matches HTHTHT and THTHTH: n(E)=2n(E)=2n(E)=2.
P(exactly one head)=24=12P(\text{exactly one head})=\dfrac{2}{4}=\dfrac12P(exactly one head)=42​=21​.
Answer: 12\dfrac1221​.
Example 6: Spinner
A spinner is numbered 111 through 888, all equally likely. Find P(greater than 5)P(\text{greater than }5)P(greater than 5).
Solution
Favorable: {6,7,8}\{6,7,8\}{6,7,8}, so n(E)=3n(E)=3n(E)=3; n(S)=8n(S)=8n(S)=8.
P=38P=\dfrac{3}{8}P=83​ (already in lowest terms).
Answer: 38\dfrac{3}{8}83​.
Example 7: Standard deck of cards
One card is drawn from a standard 525252-card deck. Find P(heart)P(\text{heart})P(heart).
Solution
There are 131313 hearts in a deck of 525252 cards.
P(heart)=1352=14P(\text{heart})=\dfrac{13}{52}=\dfrac14P(heart)=5213​=41​ (divide by the GCF, 131313).
Answer: 14\dfrac1441​.
Example 8: Complement with a jar
A jar has 666 yellow and 999 purple marbles. Find P(not yellow)P(\text{not yellow})P(not yellow).
Solution
n(S)=6+9=15n(S)=6+9=15n(S)=6+9=15. P(yellow)=615=25P(\text{yellow})=\dfrac{6}{15}=\dfrac{2}{5}P(yellow)=156​=52​.
P(not yellow)=1−25=35P(\text{not yellow})=1-\dfrac25=\dfrac35P(not yellow)=1−52​=53​.
Answer: 35\dfrac3553​.
Example 9: Three coins
Three fair coins are flipped. Find P(all three match)P(\text{all three match})P(all three match).
Solution
SSS has 23=82^3=823=8 equally likely outcomes: HHH,HHT,HTH,HTT,THH,THT,TTH,TTTHHH,HHT,HTH,HTT,THH,THT,TTH,TTTHHH,HHT,HTH,HTT,THH,THT,TTH,TTT.
"All match" means HHHHHHHHH or TTTTTTTTT: n(E)=2n(E)=2n(E)=2.
P=28=14P=\dfrac{2}{8}=\dfrac14P=82​=41​.
Answer: 14\dfrac1441​.
Example 10: Two dice — sample space grid
Two fair dice are rolled. Find P(sum=7)P(\text{sum}=7)P(sum=7).
Solution
All 36 equally likely ordered pairs from rolling two dice.
n(S)=6×6=36n(S)=6\times6=36n(S)=6×6=36. Sum of 777: (1,6),(2,5),(3,4),(4,3),(5,2),(6,1)(1,6),(2,5),(3,4),(4,3),(5,2),(6,1)(1,6),(2,5),(3,4),(4,3),(5,2),(6,1) — exactly 666 outcomes.
P(sum=7)=636=16P(\text{sum}=7)=\dfrac{6}{36}=\dfrac16P(sum=7)=366​=61​.
Answer: 16\dfrac1661​. (Sum of 777 is the single most likely sum on two dice.)
Example 11: "At least one" — complement with two dice
Two fair dice are rolled. Find P(at least one 6)P(\text{at least one }6)P(at least one 6).
Solution
Counting "at least one 666" directly is messy — use the complement: "no 666 on either die."
Each die has 555 non-666 outcomes, so n(no 6)=5×5=25n(\text{no }6)=5\times5=25n(no 6)=5×5=25 out of 363636.
P(no 6)=2536P(\text{no }6)=\dfrac{25}{36}P(no 6)=3625​, so P(at least one 6)=1−2536=1136P(\text{at least one }6)=1-\dfrac{25}{36}=\dfrac{11}{36}P(at least one 6)=1−3625​=3611​.
Answer: 1136\dfrac{11}{36}3611​.
