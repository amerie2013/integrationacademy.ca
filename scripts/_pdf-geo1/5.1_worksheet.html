5.1 Dilations
A dilation is a transformation that scales a figure from a fixed center by a scale factor kkk, producing a similar image. For a dilation centered at the origin, the rule is (x,y)↦(kx,ky)(x,y)\mapsto(kx,ky)(x,y)↦(kx,ky); for a center at (a,b)(a,b)(a,b), translate the center to the origin first: (x,y)↦(k(x−a)+a, k(y−b)+b)(x,y)\mapsto\big(k(x-a)+a,\ k(y-b)+b\big)(x,y)↦(k(x−a)+a, k(y−b)+b). When ∣k∣>1|k|>1∣k∣>1 the image is an enlargement; when 0&lt;|k|&lt;1 it is a reduction; a negative kkk places the image on the opposite side of the center, along the same line through the center and the original point. Lengths scale by a factor of ∣k∣|k|∣k∣, but areas scale by k2k^{2}k2 (since both dimensions grow). The center itself is the only fixed point — it always maps to itself. This worksheet practises dilating points and figures, finding scale factors, and reasoning about how length and area change.

Examples

Example 1: Dilate from the origin
Find the image of A(3,−2)A(3,-2)A(3,−2) under a dilation centered at the origin with k=2k=2k=2. Classify as an enlargement or reduction.
Solution
A′A'A′ is twice as far from the origin as AAA, along the same ray.
Identify the rule for an origin-centered dilation: (x,y)↦(kx,ky)(x,y)\mapsto(kx,ky)(x,y)↦(kx,ky).
Apply with k=2k=2k=2: A′=(2⋅3, 2⋅(−2))=(6,−4)A'=(2\cdot3,\ 2\cdot(-2))=(6,-4)A′=(2⋅3, 2⋅(−2))=(6,−4).
Since |k|=2&gt;1, the image is larger than the original.
Answer: A′(6,−4)A'(6,-4)A′(6,−4) — an enlargement.

Example 3: Find the scale factor
Under a dilation centered at the origin, P(4,6)P(4,6)P(4,6) maps to P′(10,15)P'(10,15)P′(10,15). Find kkk, and verify using both coordinates.
Solution
Since the dilation is centered at the origin, k=x′x=y′yk=\dfrac{x'}{x}=\dfrac{y'}{y}k=xx′​=yy′​.
Using the xxx-coordinates: k=104=52k=\dfrac{10}{4}=\dfrac{5}{2}k=410​=25​.
Check with the yyy-coordinates: k=156=52k=\dfrac{15}{6}=\dfrac{5}{2}k=615​=25​ ✓ — consistent.
Answer: k=52k=\dfrac{5}{2}k=25​.
Example 4: Dilation about a non-origin center
Dilate Q(5,3)Q(5,3)Q(5,3) about center P(1,1)P(1,1)P(1,1) with k=3k=3k=3.

Example 5: Negative scale factor
Dilate R(3,5)R(3,5)R(3,5) about the origin with k=−2k=-2k=−2. Describe the image's position relative to the center.
Solution
Apply (x,y)↦(−2x,−2y)(x,y)\mapsto(-2x,-2y)(x,y)↦(−2x,−2y): R′=(−2⋅3, −2⋅5)=(−6,−10)R'=(-2\cdot3,\ -2\cdot5)=(-6,-10)R′=(−2⋅3, −2⋅5)=(−6,−10).
R(3,5)R(3,5)R(3,5) lies northeast of the origin; R′(−6,−10)R'(-6,-10)R′(−6,−10) lies southwest of the origin — the opposite side, along the same line through the center.
Distance check: RRR is 34\sqrt{34}34​ from the origin; R′R'R′ is 136=234\sqrt{136}=2\sqrt{34}136​=234​, exactly ∣k∣=2|k|=2∣k∣=2 times as far.
Answer: R′(−6,−10)R'(-6,-10)R′(−6,−10), on the opposite side of the center, twice as far away.
Example 6: Length scales by $|k|$
A segment of length 888 is dilated with k=−1.5k=-1.5k=−1.5. Find the image length.
Solution
Length always scales by the absolute value of the scale factor, regardless of sign.
Image length =∣k∣×8=1.5×8=12=|k|\times 8=1.5\times8=12=∣k∣×8=1.5×8=12.
Answer: 121212.
Example 7: Area scales by $k^{2}$
A square has side length 444 (area 161616). It is dilated with k=3k=3k=3. Find the new side length, new area, and the ratio of the new area to the original. Compare that ratio to k2k^{2}k2.
Solution
New side length =4×3=12=4\times3=12=4×3=12.
New area =122=144=12^{2}=144=122=144.
Ratio of areas =14416=9=\dfrac{144}{16}=9=16144​=9.
Compare: k2=32=9k^{2}=3^{2}=9k2=32=9 — matches exactly.
Conclusion: area scales by k2k^{2}k2, not kkk, because both dimensions of the square are multiplied by kkk.
Example 8: The fixed point
A dilation is centered at C(−2,5)C(-2,5)C(−2,5). What is the image of CCC itself, no matter what kkk is?
Solution
By definition, the center of a dilation is the one point that never moves — every other point scales relative to it, but the center's distance to itself is always 000, and 0×k=00\times k=00×k=0.
Answer: CCC maps to itself: (−2,5)(-2,5)(−2,5).
Example 9: Dilation preserves direction (parallel sides)
Triangle has vertices A(1,1)A(1,1)A(1,1), B(4,1)B(4,1)B(4,1), C(1,5)C(1,5)C(1,5). It is dilated about the origin with k=2k=2k=2. Verify that B′C′‾\overline{B'C'}B′C′ is parallel to BC‾\overline{BC}BC by comparing slopes.
Solution
Dilating about the origin with k=2k=2k=2 doubles every coordinate.
Find the image points: A′(2,2)A'(2,2)A′(2,2), B′(8,2)B'(8,2)B′(8,2), C′(2,10)C'(2,10)C′(2,10).
Slope of BC‾\overline{BC}BC: 5−11−4=4−3=−43\dfrac{5-1}{1-4}=\dfrac{4}{-3}=-\dfrac{4}{3}1−45−1​=−34​=−34​.
Slope of B′C′‾\overline{B'C'}B′C′: 10−22−8=8−6=−43\dfrac{10-2}{2-8}=\dfrac{8}{-6}=-\dfrac{4}{3}2−810−2​=−68​=−34​.
The slopes match exactly.
Conclusion: B′C′‾∥BC‾\overline{B'C'}\parallel\overline{BC}B′C′∥BC — dilations preserve the direction of every side, which is why the image is always similar to the original.
Example 10: Blueprint scale factor
An architect's blueprint uses scale factor k=150k=\dfrac{1}{50}k=501​ (from the real building to the drawing). A wall is 200200200 units long on the actual building. Find its length on the blueprint, and find the scale factor to convert blueprint measurements back to real ones.
Solution
Blueprint length =k×(real length)=150×200=4=k\times(\text{real length})=\dfrac{1}{50}\times200=4=k×(real length)=501​×200=4 units.
The reverse dilation (blueprint →\to→ real) uses the reciprocal scale factor: 11/50=50\dfrac{1}{1/50}=501/501​=50.
Check: 4×50=2004\times50=2004×50=200 ✓, back to the original length.
Answer: Blueprint length =4=4=4 units; reverse scale factor =50=50=50.
