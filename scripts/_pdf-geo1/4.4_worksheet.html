4.4 ASA and AAS Congruence
The ASA Postulate proves triangles congruent from two pairs of congruent angles plus the included side (the side between those two angles). The AAS Theorem proves congruence from two pairs of congruent angles plus a non-included side — a side that lies opposite one of the given angles rather than between them. AAS actually reduces to ASA: since two angles are congruent, the Angle-Sum Theorem forces the third pair of angles to be congruent too, which makes the given side included relative to that third angle. Note that AAA is never a valid congruence criterion — matching all three angles only guarantees the triangles are the same shape (similar), not the same size. This worksheet applies ASA and AAS with numeric, algebraic, and overlapping-triangle reasoning, and contrasts them with AAA.

Examples

Example 1: Apply ASA
In △ABC\triangle ABC△ABC and △DEF\triangle DEF△DEF, ∠A≅∠D\angle A \cong \angle D∠A≅∠D, AB=DEAB = DEAB=DE (the side between ∠A\angle A∠A and ∠B\angle B∠B), and ∠B≅∠E\angle B \cong \angle E∠B≅∠E. What can you conclude?
Solution
Two angles and the side directly between them are marked congruent.
Identify the given parts: ∠A≅∠D\angle A \cong \angle D∠A≅∠D, ∠B≅∠E\angle B \cong \angle E∠B≅∠E, and side AB‾\overline{AB}AB lies between ∠A\angle A∠A and ∠B\angle B∠B — it is the included side.
Two angles and the included side are congruent.
Conclusion: △ABC≅△DEF\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEF△ABC≅△DEF by ASA.
Example 2: Apply AAS
In △ABC\triangle ABC△ABC and △DEF\triangle DEF△DEF, ∠A≅∠D\angle A \cong \angle D∠A≅∠D, ∠B≅∠E\angle B \cong \angle E∠B≅∠E, and BC=EFBC = EFBC=EF. Is BC‾\overline{BC}BC included between the two given angles? What can you conclude?
Solution
Side AB‾\overline{AB}AB (not BC‾\overline{BC}BC) lies between ∠A\angle A∠A and ∠B\angle B∠B, so BC‾\overline{BC}BC is not included — it lies opposite ∠A\angle A∠A.
We have two pairs of congruent angles and one pair of congruent non-included sides.
Conclusion: △ABC≅△DEF\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEF△ABC≅△DEF by AAS.
Example 3: Why AAS actually works
Explain why AAS guarantees congruence, using the Angle-Sum Theorem.
Solution
Given ∠A≅∠D\angle A \cong \angle D∠A≅∠D and ∠B≅∠E\angle B \cong \angle E∠B≅∠E, apply the Angle-Sum Theorem to both triangles:
∠C=180−∠A−∠B\angle C = 180-\angle A-\angle B∠C=180−∠A−∠B and ∠F=180−∠D−∠E\angle F=180-\angle D-\angle E∠F=180−∠D−∠E.
Since ∠A=∠D\angle A=\angle D∠A=∠D and ∠B=∠E\angle B=\angle E∠B=∠E, the right-hand sides are equal, so ∠C≅∠F\angle C \cong \angle F∠C≅∠F as well.
Now side BC‾\overline{BC}BC is included between ∠B\angle B∠B and the newly-shown ∠C\angle C∠C (and likewise EF‾\overline{EF}EF between ∠E\angle E∠E and ∠F\angle F∠F).
Conclusion: AAS is really ASA in disguise — the third angle pair is congruent "for free" from the Angle-Sum Theorem, making the given side included after all.

Example 5: Shared side via angle bisector (AAS)
BD‾\overline{BD}BD bisects ∠ABC\angle ABC∠ABC (so ∠ABD≅∠CBD\angle ABD \cong \angle CBD∠ABD≅∠CBD), and ∠A≅∠C\angle A \cong \angle C∠A≅∠C. Prove △ABD≅△CBD\triangle ABD \cong \triangle CBD△ABD≅△CBD.
Solution
BD‾\overline{BD}BD splits the triangle; the shared side BDBDBD is opposite the marked angles at AAA and CCC.
Identify the parts: ∠A≅∠C\angle A \cong \angle C∠A≅∠C (given) and ∠ABD≅∠CBD\angle ABD \cong \angle CBD∠ABD≅∠CBD (bisector, given) — two pairs of angles.
BD‾≅BD‾\overline{BD} \cong \overline{BD}BD≅BD by the Reflexive Property (shared side).
In each triangle, BD‾\overline{BD}BD is opposite ∠A\angle A∠A (or ∠C\angle C∠C) — it is not between the two given angles, so it is a non-included side.
Conclusion: △ABD≅△CBD\triangle ABD \cong \triangle CBD△ABD≅△CBD by AAS.
Example 6: AAA is not enough
△ABC\triangle ABC△ABC has angles 40∘40^{\circ}40∘, 60∘60^{\circ}60∘, 80∘80^{\circ}80∘ and sides 555, 777, 888. △DEF\triangle DEF△DEF has the same three angles 40∘40^{\circ}40∘, 60∘60^{\circ}60∘, 80∘80^{\circ}80∘ but sides 101010, 141414, 161616. Are the triangles congruent?
Solution
Compare the angles: all three pairs match exactly (40∘=40∘40^{\circ}=40^{\circ}40∘=40∘, 60∘=60∘60^{\circ}=60^{\circ}60∘=60∘, 80∘=80∘80^{\circ}=80^{\circ}80∘=80∘) — this is AAA.
Compare the sides: 5≠105\neq105=10, 7≠147\neq147=14, 8≠168\neq168=16 — none of the corresponding sides match.
The triangles are the same shape (each side of △DEF\triangle DEF△DEF is exactly double the corresponding side of △ABC\triangle ABC△ABC) but not the same size.
Conclusion: Not congruent — matching angles only (AAA) proves the triangles are similar, never congruence on its own.
Example 7: CPCTC after ASA
△ABC≅△DEF\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEF△ABC≅△DEF by ASA, with AB=10AB=10AB=10, BC=14BC=14BC=14, and ∠B=72∘\angle B=72^{\circ}∠B=72∘. Find DEDEDE, EFEFEF, and ∠E\angle E∠E.
Solution
The correspondence from △ABC≅△DEF\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEF△ABC≅△DEF is A↔DA\leftrightarrow DA↔D, B↔EB\leftrightarrow EB↔E, C↔FC\leftrightarrow FC↔F.
By CPCTC: DE=AB=10DE=AB=10DE=AB=10, EF=BC=14EF=BC=14EF=BC=14, and ∠E=∠B=72∘\angle E=\angle B=72^{\circ}∠E=∠B=72∘.
Answer: DE=10DE=10DE=10, EF=14EF=14EF=14, ∠E=72∘\angle E=72^{\circ}∠E=72∘.
Example 8: Roof brackets (ASA in context)
Two triangular roof brackets each have base angles 55∘55^{\circ}55∘ and 80∘80^{\circ}80∘ with the included side (between them) measuring 121212 in. Are the brackets congruent? Find the third angle of each.
Solution
Each bracket is a triangle with two known base angles and the side between them.
Both brackets have ∠1=55∘\angle 1 = 55^{\circ}∠1=55∘, ∠2=80∘\angle 2=80^{\circ}∠2=80∘, and the included side =12=12=12 in — matching ASA information.
Find the third angle using the Angle-Sum Theorem: 180−55−80=45∘180-55-80=45^{\circ}180−55−80=45∘.
Conclusion: the brackets are congruent by ASA; each has a third angle of 45∘45^{\circ}45∘.
