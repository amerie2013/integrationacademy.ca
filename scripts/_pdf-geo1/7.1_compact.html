7.1 Polygon Angle Sums
Every convex polygon's interior angles can be found by splitting the polygon into triangles from one vertex: an nnn-gon splits into (n−2)(n-2)(n−2) triangles, and since each triangle contributes 180∘180^\circ180∘, the interior angle sum is S=(n−2)⋅180∘S=(n-2)\cdot180^\circS=(n−2)⋅180∘. Exterior angles (one per vertex, formed by extending each side) always add to exactly 360∘360^\circ360∘ for any convex polygon, no matter how many sides it has — walking once around the perimeter turns you through one full rotation. For a regular polygon (congruent sides and angles), split these totals evenly: each interior angle is (n−2)180∘n\dfrac{(n-2)180^\circ}{n}n(n−2)180∘​, and each exterior angle is 360∘n\dfrac{360^\circ}{n}n360∘​. At any single vertex, the interior and exterior angles are supplementary.
