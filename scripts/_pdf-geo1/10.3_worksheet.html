10.3 Density Problems
Density links geometry to real-world materials: ρ=mV\rho=\dfrac{m}{V}ρ=Vm​ (mass per unit volume), so m=ρVm=\rho Vm=ρV and V=mρV=\dfrac{m}{\rho}V=ρm​ — the same three-way relationship as distance/rate/time. A separate but related idea, population density, is people per unit area: D=PAD=\dfrac{P}{A}D=AP​. Most density problems here are two-step: first use a volume formula from this unit to find VVV (or B⋅hB\cdot hB⋅h), then apply the density relationship. This worksheet also covers comparing densities (which object is denser, or would float in water, since water ≈1 g/cm3\approx1\text{ g/cm}^3≈1 g/cm3), weighted-average density of composite objects, and unit conversions.

Examples

Example 1: Density from mass and volume
A solid has mass 240240240 g and volume 303030 cm3^33. Find its density.
Solution
Identify the formula: density is mass per unit volume, ρ=mV\rho=\dfrac{m}{V}ρ=Vm​.
Compute: ρ=24030=8\rho=\dfrac{240}{30}=8ρ=30240​=8.
Answer: 888 g/cm3^33.
Example 2: Mass from density (cube)
A cube of edge 444 cm has density 2.52.52.5 g/cm3^33. Find its mass.
Solution
First find the volume: V=s3=43=64V=s^{3}=4^{3}=64V=s3=43=64 cm3^33.
Then m=ρV=2.5×64=160m=\rho V=2.5\times64=160m=ρV=2.5×64=160 g.
Answer: 160160160 g.
Example 3: Volume from mass and density
An object has mass 909090 g and density 333 g/cm3^33. Find its volume.
Solution
Identify: rearrange ρ=mV\rho=\dfrac{m}{V}ρ=Vm​ to solve for volume instead of density: V=mρV=\dfrac{m}{\rho}V=ρm​.
Compute: V=903=30V=\dfrac{90}{3}=30V=390​=30.
Answer: 303030 cm3^33.
Example 4: Cylinder — find volume, then density
A metal cylinder has radius 333 cm, height 555 cm, and mass 450π450\pi450π g. Find its density.
Solution
Find the cylinder's volume first, then divide mass by volume.
Step 1 — volume: V=πr2h=π(9)(5)=45πV=\pi r^{2}h=\pi(9)(5)=45\piV=πr2h=π(9)(5)=45π cm3^33.
Step 2 — density: ρ=mV=450π45π\rho=\dfrac{m}{V}=\dfrac{450\pi}{45\pi}ρ=Vm​=45π450π​. The π\piπ cancels: ρ=10\rho=10ρ=10 g/cm3^33.
Answer: 101010 g/cm3^33.
Example 5: Sphere — find volume, then density
A metal ball bearing (sphere) has radius 333 cm and mass 288π288\pi288π g. Find its density.
Solution
Sphere volume first, then ρ=m/V\rho=m/Vρ=m/V.
Step 1 — volume: V=43πr3=43π(27)=36πV=\dfrac43\pi r^{3}=\dfrac43\pi(27)=36\piV=34​πr3=34​π(27)=36π cm3^33.
Step 2 — density: ρ=mV=288π36π=8\rho=\dfrac{m}{V}=\dfrac{288\pi}{36\pi}=8ρ=Vm​=36π288π​=8 g/cm3^33.
Answer: 888 g/cm3^33.
Example 6: Does it float? (compare to water)
A block has mass 540540540 g and volume 600600600 cm3^33. Find its density and determine whether it floats in water (water has density 111 g/cm3^33).
Solution
Compute the density: ρ=540600=0.9\rho=\dfrac{540}{600}=0.9ρ=600540​=0.9 g/cm3^33.
Compare to water: since 0.9<10.9<10.9<1, the block is less dense than water, so it floats.
Answer: ρ=0.9\rho=0.9ρ=0.9 g/cm3^33; it floats.
Example 7: Population density
A town of 48,00048{,}00048,000 people covers 161616 km2^22. Find the population density.
Solution
Identify the formula: population density is people per unit area, D=PAD=\dfrac{P}{A}D=AP​.
Compute: D=48,00016=3000D=\dfrac{48{,}000}{16}=3000D=1648,000​=3000.
Answer: 300030003000 people per km2^22.
Example 8: Population from area and density
A region has area 252525 mi2^22 and population density 200200200 people/mi2^22. Find the population.
Solution
Identify: rearrange D=PAD=\dfrac{P}{A}D=AP​ to solve for population instead of density: P=D×AP=D\times AP=D×A.
Compute: P=200×25=5000P=200\times25=5000P=200×25=5000.
Answer: 500050005000 people.
Example 9: Area from population and density
A city has population 900090009000 and population density 300300300 people/km2^22. Find its area.
Solution
Identify: rearrange D=PAD=\dfrac{P}{A}D=AP​ to solve for area instead: A=PDA=\dfrac{P}{D}A=DP​.
Compute: A=9000300=30A=\dfrac{9000}{300}=30A=3009000​=30.
Answer: 303030 km2^22.
Example 10: Composite object — weighted-average density
An object is cast from two materials fused together: Part 1 has volume 202020 cm3^33 and density 888 g/cm3^33; Part 2 has volume 303030 cm3^33 and density 333 g/cm3^33. Find the object's overall (average) density.
Solution
Step 1 — mass of each part: m1=ρ1V1=8(20)=160m_1=\rho_1V_1=8(20)=160m1​=ρ1​V1​=8(20)=160 g;   m2=ρ2V2=3(30)=90m_2=\rho_2V_2=3(30)=90m2​=ρ2​V2​=3(30)=90 g.
Step 2 — totals: total mass =160+90=250=160+90=250=160+90=250 g; total volume =20+30=50=20+30=50=20+30=50 cm3^33.
Step 3 — overall density: ρavg=25050=5\rho_{\text{avg}}=\dfrac{250}{50}=5ρavg​=50250​=5 g/cm3^33.
Note: the average density is not simply 8+32=5.5\dfrac{8+3}{2}=5.528+3​=5.5 — it must be weighted by volume, since the two parts don't have equal volume in general (here it happened close, but always compute total mass over total volume).
Answer: 555 g/cm3^33.
Example 11: Unit conversion
Aluminum has density 2.72.72.7 g/cm3^33. Convert this to kg/m3^33.
Solution
Convert the units piece by piece: 111 g =0.001=0.001=0.001 kg, and 111 m3=(100 cm)3=1,000,000^3=(100\text{ cm})^{3}=1{,}000{,}0003=(100 cm)3=1,000,000 cm3^33.
2.7 gcm3×0.001 kg1 g×1,000,000 cm31 m3=2.7×1000=27002.7\ \dfrac{\text{g}}{\text{cm}^{3}} \times \dfrac{0.001\text{ kg}}{1\text{ g}} \times \dfrac{1{,}000{,}000\text{ cm}^3}{1\text{ m}^3} = 2.7\times1000 = 27002.7 cm3g​×1 g0.001 kg​×1 m31,000,000 cm3​=2.7×1000=2700 kg/m3^33.
Shortcut: 1 g/cm3=1000 kg/m31\text{ g/cm}^3 = 1000\text{ kg/m}^31 g/cm3=1000 kg/m3 always, so just multiply by 100010001000.
Answer: 270027002700 kg/m3^33.
