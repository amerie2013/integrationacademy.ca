2.4 Parallel & Perpendicular Equations
Slope connects the visual idea of parallel and perpendicular lines to hard algebra: equal slopes force lines to run in the same direction forever without meeting, while a slope and its negative reciprocal force a perfect 90∘90^\circ90∘ turn. This section goes beyond just flipping a fraction — it uses the slope formula m=y2−y1x2−x1m=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}m=x2​−x1​y2​−y1​​ from two raw points, tests whether three points are collinear, and solves for a missing coordinate that makes two lines parallel or perpendicular.

Examples

Example 1: Why parallel lines share slope
A line has slope 333. Explain why any line parallel to it must also have slope 333, then state that slope.
Solution
Slope measures steepness — the rise-to-run ratio never changes along a straight line.
Slope measures a line's steepness and direction; two distinct lines are parallel exactly when they never meet, which requires identical steepness.
If the slopes differed even slightly, the lines would eventually converge or diverge and cross somewhere.
Answer: The parallel line's slope is also 333.
Example 2: Perpendicular slopes — negative reciprocal
A line has slope 222. Find the slope of a line perpendicular to it, and explain the negative reciprocal rule.
Solution
Perpendicular lines: slopes multiply to −1.
Rule: for two non-vertical, non-horizontal lines, perpendicular slopes multiply to −1-1−1: m1⋅m2=−1m_1 \cdot m_2 = -1m1​⋅m2​=−1.
Solve for the perpendicular slope: 2⋅m2=−1⇒m2=−122 \cdot m_2 = -1 \Rightarrow m_2 = -\dfrac{1}{2}2⋅m2​=−1⇒m2​=−21​.
Shortcut: flip the fraction and switch the sign — the "negative reciprocal."
Answer: −12-\dfrac{1}{2}−21​.
Example 3: Negative reciprocal of a fraction
A line has slope −34-\dfrac{3}{4}−43​. Find the perpendicular slope.
Solution
Flip the fraction and switch the sign: −34→43-\dfrac{3}{4} \rightarrow \dfrac{4}{3}−43​→34​.
Check: (−34)(43)=−1\left(-\dfrac{3}{4}\right)\left(\dfrac{4}{3}\right) = -1(−43​)(34​)=−1. ✓
Answer: 43\dfrac{4}{3}34​.
Example 4: Slope from two points, then a parallel line
Find the slope of the line through (1,2)(1,2)(1,2) and (5,10)(5,10)(5,10), then write the equation of a line parallel to it through (0,−3)(0,-3)(0,−3).
Solution
Two points determine both the slope and the direction of the line.
Apply the slope formula: m=y2−y1x2−x1=10−25−1=84=2m = \dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1} = \dfrac{10-2}{5-1} = \dfrac{8}{4} = 2m=x2​−x1​y2​−y1​​=5−110−2​=48​=2.
A parallel line keeps the same slope, m=2m=2m=2, and uses the new yyy-intercept −3-3−3: y=2x−3y = 2x - 3y=2x−3.
Answer: slope =2=2=2; parallel line: y=2x−3y = 2x - 3y=2x−3.
Example 5: Perpendicular through a point
Write the equation of a line perpendicular to y=−13x+4y = -\dfrac{1}{3}x + 4y=−31​x+4 through (0,2)(0, 2)(0,2).
Solution
Find the perpendicular slope: negative reciprocal of −13-\dfrac{1}{3}−31​ is 333.
Use the given point as the yyy-intercept since it has x=0x=0x=0: b=2b = 2b=2.
Answer: y=3x+2y = 3x + 2y=3x+2.
Example 6: Identify parallel from equations
Are y=5x−1y = 5x - 1y=5x−1 and y=5x+7y = 5x + 7y=5x+7 parallel?
Solution
Compare slopes directly from slope-intercept form: both have slope 555.
Same slope, different yyy-intercepts (−1-1−1 vs. 777) means the lines are distinct but never meet.
Answer: Yes — parallel.
Example 7: Identify perpendicular from equations
Are y=4xy = 4xy=4x and y=−14x+1y = -\dfrac{1}{4}x + 1y=−41​x+1 perpendicular?
Solution
Multiply the slopes: 4×(−14)=−14 \times \left(-\dfrac{1}{4}\right) = -14×(−41​)=−1.
The product is exactly −1-1−1, which is the perpendicular condition.
Answer: Yes — perpendicular.
Example 8: Horizontal and vertical special cases
State the slope of y=5y = 5y=5 and the slope of x=3x = 3x=3, and explain why the usual product rule can't test them against each other.
Solution
y=5y=5y=5 is horizontal: slope =0=0=0 (rise is always 000). x=3x=3x=3 is vertical: slope is undefined (run is always 000, causing division by zero).
The rule m1m2=−1m_1m_2=-1m1​m2​=−1 needs two actual numbers to multiply — "undefined" isn't a number, so the rule can't be applied directly, even though a horizontal and a vertical line ARE always perpendicular by definition.
Answer: slope of y=5y=5y=5 is 000; slope of x=3x=3x=3 is undefined; they are perpendicular by definition, not by the product rule.
Example 9: Are three points collinear?
Determine whether A(1,2)A(1,2)A(1,2), B(3,6)B(3,6)B(3,6), and C(5,10)C(5,10)C(5,10) are collinear.
Solution
Equal slopes through a shared point force all three onto one line.
Compute the slope between the first two points: mAB=6−23−1=42=2m_{AB} = \dfrac{6-2}{3-1} = \dfrac{4}{2} = 2mAB​=3−16−2​=24​=2.
Compute the slope between the last two points: mBC=10−65−3=42=2m_{BC} = \dfrac{10-6}{5-3} = \dfrac{4}{2} = 2mBC​=5−310−6​=24​=2.
Equal slopes through the shared point BBB mean all three points lie on the same line.
Answer: Yes, AAA, BBB, CCC are collinear.
Example 10: Solve for a coordinate using perpendicularity
A line through (2,1)(2,1)(2,1) and (k,7)(k,7)(k,7) is perpendicular to a line with slope −13-\dfrac{1}{3}−31​. Find kkk.
Solution
Perpendicular to −13-\dfrac{1}{3}−31​ means the target slope is the negative reciprocal: 333.
Set the slope formula for the two given points equal to 333: 7−1k−2=3\dfrac{7-1}{k-2} = 3k−27−1​=3.
6k−2=3⇒6=3(k−2)⇒6=3k−6⇒12=3k⇒k=4\dfrac{6}{k-2}=3 \Rightarrow 6 = 3(k-2) \Rightarrow 6 = 3k-6 \Rightarrow 12 = 3k \Rightarrow k = 4k−26​=3⇒6=3(k−2)⇒6=3k−6⇒12=3k⇒k=4.
Check: slope from (2,1)(2,1)(2,1) to (4,7)(4,7)(4,7) is 62=3\dfrac{6}{2}=326​=3, and 3×(−13)=−13 \times \left(-\dfrac{1}{3}\right) = -13×(−31​)=−1. ✓
