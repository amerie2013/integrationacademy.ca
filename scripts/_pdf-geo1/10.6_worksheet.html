10.6 Surface Area and Volume Under Dilation
When a solid is dilated (scaled) by factor kkk, every linear measurement (edges, radii, heights, slant heights) scales by k1=kk^{1}=kk1=k. Surface area is built from products of two lengths (length ×\times× width), so it scales by k2k^{2}k2; volume is built from products of three lengths, so it scales by k3k^{3}k3. This is why doubling a solid's size (k=2k=2k=2) quadruples its surface area but multiplies its volume by 888 — a fact that shows up everywhere from packaging costs to biology (why small animals lose heat faster than large ones, since SA scales slower than volume). This worksheet computes new SA/volume after a dilation, recovers kkk from a given ratio, and converts between SA ratios and volume ratios.

Examples

Example 1: Scale surface area — and see why $k^2$
A cube has surface area 545454. It is dilated by scale factor k=2k=2k=2. Find the new surface area.
Solution
Dilating a solid by kkk scales every length by kkk, so area (length × length) scales by k2k^2k2.
Every edge doubles, and SA is built from edge ×\times× edge terms, so SA scales by k2=22=4k^{2}=2^{2}=4k2=22=4.
New SA =54×4=216=54\times4=216=54×4=216.
Check with formula directly: original edge sss satisfies 6s2=54⇒s2=9⇒s=36s^{2}=54\Rightarrow s^{2}=9\Rightarrow s=36s2=54⇒s2=9⇒s=3. New edge =6=6=6, new SA =6(62)=216=6(6^{2})=216=6(62)=216. ✓
Answer: 216216216.
Example 2: Scale volume — and see why $k^3$
A sphere has volume 36π36\pi36π. It is dilated by scale factor k=3k=3k=3. Find the new volume.
Solution
Volume is built from three length factors (r×r×rr\times r\times rr×r×r), so it scales by k3=33=27k^{3}=3^{3}=27k3=33=27.
New V=36π×27=972πV=36\pi\times27=972\piV=36π×27=972π.
Answer: 972π972\pi972π.
Example 3: Find $k$ from an SA ratio
Two similar solids have surface areas in ratio 9:19:19:1 (large to small). Find the scale factor kkk (large to small).
Solution
SA ratio =k2=k^{2}=k2, so k2=9k^{2}=9k2=9.
k=9=3k=\sqrt9=3k=9​=3 (take the positive root — scale factors are positive).
Answer: k=3k=3k=3.
Example 4: Find $k$ from a volume ratio
Two similar solids have volumes in ratio 8:18:18:1 (large to small). Find kkk.
Solution
Volume ratio =k3=k^{3}=k3, so k3=8k^{3}=8k3=8.
k=83=2k=\sqrt[3]{8}=2k=38​=2.
Answer: k=2k=2k=2.
Example 5: Scale both SA and volume together
A prism has SA =40=40=40 and volume =30=30=30. It is dilated by k=12k=\dfrac12k=21​ (shrunk). Find the new SA and new volume.
Solution
Identify the scale factor: k=12k=\dfrac12k=21​. Surface area scales by k2k^{2}k2; volume scales by k3k^{3}k3.
Apply each scaling rule to the original measurements:
New SA =40×k2=40×14=10=40\times k^{2}=40\times\dfrac14=10=40×k2=40×41​=10.
New V=30×k3=30×18=308=154V=30\times k^{3}=30\times\dfrac18=\dfrac{30}{8}=\dfrac{15}{4}V=30×k3=30×81​=830​=415​.
Both measurements shrink, but volume shrinks faster than surface area since it scales by a higher power of kkk.
Answer: new SA =10=10=10; new V=154V=\dfrac{15}{4}V=415​.
Example 6: Edge length and SA together
A cube has edge 444 and SA =96=96=96. After a dilation with k=32k=\dfrac32k=23​, find the new edge and new SA.
Solution
Edge scales by kkk; SA scales by k2k^2k2.
New edge =4×32=6=4\times\dfrac32=6=4×23​=6.
New SA =96×(32)2=96×94=216=96\times\left(\dfrac32\right)^{2}=96\times\dfrac94=216=96×(23​)2=96×49​=216.
Check: 6(62)=2166(6^{2})=2166(62)=216. ✓
Answer: new edge =6=6=6; new SA =216=216=216.
Example 7: Volume ratio from a given $k$
Two similar cones have scale factor k=4k=4k=4 (large to small). Find the volume ratio (new : old).
Solution
Volume ratio =k3=43=64=k^{3}=4^{3}=64=k3=43=64.
Answer: 64:164:164:1.
Example 8: SA ratio from a volume ratio
Two similar solids have volume ratio 27:827:827:8. Find the SA ratio.
Solution
First find kkk from the volume ratio: k3=278⇒k=2783=32k^{3}=\dfrac{27}{8}\Rightarrow k=\sqrt[3]{\dfrac{27}{8}}=\dfrac{3}{2}k3=827​⇒k=3827​​=23​.
Then SA ratio =k2=(32)2=94=k^{2}=\left(\dfrac32\right)^{2}=\dfrac94=k2=(23​)2=49​.
Answer: SA ratio =94=\dfrac94=49​, i.e. 9:49:49:4.
Example 9: Shrinking a cylinder
A cylinder has volume 200π200\pi200π. It is dilated by k=12k=\dfrac12k=21​. Find the new volume.
Solution
Identify the scale factor: k=12k=\dfrac12k=21​. Volume scales by k3k^{3}k3.
Apply the scaling rule:
New V=200π×(12)3=200π×18=25πV=200\pi\times\left(\dfrac12\right)^{3}=200\pi\times\dfrac18=25\piV=200π×(21​)3=200π×81​=25π.
Shrinking every length by half shrinks the volume to just 18\dfrac1881​ of the original.
Answer: 25π25\pi25π.
Example 10: Chain reasoning — volume ratio to $k$ to new SA
A sphere has surface area 144π144\pi144π and volume 288π288\pi288π. A similar (larger) sphere has volume 2304π2304\pi2304π. Find the scale factor kkk, then the larger sphere's surface area.
Solution
First recover kkk from the volume ratio, then apply k2k^2k2 to the SA.
Step 1 — find kkk from the volume ratio: k3=2304π288π=8  ⇒  k=83=2k^{3}=\dfrac{2304\pi}{288\pi}=8 \;\Rightarrow\; k=\sqrt[3]{8}=2k3=288π2304π​=8⇒k=38​=2.
Step 2 — SA ratio: k2=4k^{2}=4k2=4.
Step 3 — new SA: 144π×4=576π144\pi\times4=576\pi144π×4=576π.
Answer: k=2k=2k=2; new SA =576π=576\pi=576π.
Example 11: Real-world application — similar water tanks
A small water tank holds 888 L and needs 222 m2^22 of material to build. A larger, similar tank has a linear scale factor of k=3k=3k=3 compared to the small one. Find the large tank's capacity and the material needed to build it.
Solution
Capacity (volume) scales by k3k^{3}k3: 8×33=8×27=2168\times3^{3}=8\times27=2168×33=8×27=216 L.
Material (surface area) scales by k2k^{2}k2: 2×32=2×9=182\times3^{2}=2\times9=182×32=2×9=18 m2^22.
Answer: capacity =216=216=216 L; material needed =18=18=18 m2^22. Notice the tank holds 27×27\times27× as much water but needs only 9×9\times9× the material — bigger containers are more material-efficient per liter.
