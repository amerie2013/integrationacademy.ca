9.3 Chords, Tangents, and Secants
Several clean theorems govern how chords, tangents, and secants interact with a circle. A radius drawn perpendicular to a chord always bisects it (and the converse: the perpendicular from the center to a chord bisects it), which lets the Pythagorean Theorem connect radius, chord, and distance-to-center. A tangent line is always perpendicular to the radius at the point of tangency, and the two tangent segments drawn from the same external point are always congruent. For intersecting chords, secants, and tangents, two families of theorems apply: length theorems (chord–chord: a⋅b=c⋅da\cdot b=c\cdot da⋅b=c⋅d; tangent–secant: t2=(whole)(external)t^2=(\text{whole})(\text{external})t2=(whole)(external); secant–secant: (whole1)(external1)=(whole2)(external2)(\text{whole}_1)(\text{external}_1)=(\text{whole}_2)(\text{external}_2)(whole1​)(external1​)=(whole2​)(external2​) — together called the Power of a Point) and angle theorems (two chords meeting inside: angle =12=\dfrac{1}{2}=21​(sum of intercepted arcs); two secants/tangents meeting outside: angle =12=\dfrac{1}{2}=21​(difference of intercepted arcs)). This worksheet uses all of these, including multi-secant algebra and a real-world tangent application.

Examples

Example 1: Perpendicular radius bisects the chord
A circle has radius 131313. A chord is 555 units from the center. Find the chord's length.
Solution
The radius perpendicular to the chord bisects it, forming a right triangle.
The perpendicular from the center, the half-chord, and the radius form a right triangle:
half-chord =132−52=169−25=144=12=\sqrt{13^2-5^2}=\sqrt{169-25}=\sqrt{144}=12=132−52​=169−25​=144​=12.
Full chord =2(12)=24=2(12)=24=2(12)=24.
Answer: 242424.
Example 2: Congruent tangent segments
From external point PPP, two tangent segments to a circle measure 3x+23x+23x+2 and 232323. Find xxx.
Solution
Two tangent segments from the same external point are always congruent.
Tangent segments from the same external point are congruent:
3x+2=233x+2=233x+2=23
3x=21⇒x=73x=21 \Rightarrow x=73x=21⇒x=7.
Answer: x=7x=7x=7.
Example 3: Chord–chord product
Two chords intersect inside a circle. One chord is split into segments 666 and 121212; the other into segments 888 and xxx. Find xxx.
Solution
The products of the segments of intersecting chords are equal:
6⋅12=8x6\cdot12=8x6⋅12=8x
72=8x⇒x=972=8x \Rightarrow x=972=8x⇒x=9.
Answer: 999.
Example 4: Tangent–secant length
A tangent segment has length ttt. A secant from the same external point has external part 444 and whole length 161616. Find ttt.
Solution
Identify the relationship: for a tangent and secant from the same external point, the tangent squared equals the secant's whole length times its external part: t2=(whole)(external)t^2=(\text{whole})(\text{external})t2=(whole)(external).
Compute: t2=16⋅4=64t^2=16\cdot4=64t2=16⋅4=64, so t=64=8t=\sqrt{64}=8t=64​=8.
Answer: 888.
Example 5: Exterior angle from two secants
Two secants from an external point intercept arcs of 100∘100^\circ100∘ and 30∘30^\circ30∘. Find the angle at the external point.
Solution
Exterior angle =12(far arc−near arc)=12(100∘−30∘)=12(70∘)=35∘=\dfrac{1}{2}(\text{far arc}-\text{near arc})=\dfrac{1}{2}(100^\circ-30^\circ)=\dfrac{1}{2}(70^\circ)=35^\circ=21​(far arc−near arc)=21​(100∘−30∘)=21​(70∘)=35∘.
Answer: 35∘35^\circ35∘.
Example 6: Distance from center to a chord
A circle has radius 101010 and a chord of length 161616. Find the distance from the center to the chord.
Solution
Identify: the perpendicular from the center bisects the chord, forming a right triangle with legs equal to the half-chord and the unknown distance, and hypotenuse equal to the radius.
Half-chord =162=8=\dfrac{16}{2}=8=216​=8. Apply the Pythagorean Theorem: distance =102−82=100−64=36=6=\sqrt{10^2-8^2}=\sqrt{100-64}=\sqrt{36}=6=102−82​=100−64​=36​=6.
Answer: 666.
Example 7: Tangent segments — algebra
Tangent segments from an external point measure 5x−35x-35x−3 and 272727. Find xxx.
Solution
Identify: the two tangent segments come from the same external point, so they must be congruent.
Set them equal and solve: 5x−3=27⇒5x=30⇒x=65x-3=27 \Rightarrow 5x=30 \Rightarrow x=65x−3=27⇒5x=30⇒x=6.
Answer: x=6x=6x=6 (each tangent segment then measures 272727).
Example 8: Interior angle from two chords
Two chords intersect inside a circle, intercepting arcs of 95∘95^\circ95∘ and 55∘55^\circ55∘. Find the angle formed.
Solution
Interior angle =12(sum of intercepted arcs)=12(95∘+55∘)=12(150∘)=75∘=\dfrac{1}{2}(\text{sum of intercepted arcs})=\dfrac{1}{2}(95^\circ+55^\circ)=\dfrac{1}{2}(150^\circ)=75^\circ=21​(sum of intercepted arcs)=21​(95∘+55∘)=21​(150∘)=75∘.
Answer: 75∘75^\circ75∘.
Example 9: Tangent–secant, solve for the whole secant
A tangent segment is 121212; a secant from the same external point has external segment 888. Find the whole length of the secant.
Solution
Identify: use the tangent–secant relationship t2=(whole)(external)t^2=(\text{whole})(\text{external})t2=(whole)(external), this time solving for the whole length instead of the tangent.
Compute: 122=8w⇒144=8w⇒w=1812^2=8w \Rightarrow 144=8w \Rightarrow w=18122=8w⇒144=8w⇒w=18.
Answer: 181818.
Example 10: Two secants — algebra with the Power of a Point
From external point PPP, one secant has near (external) segment 333 and far segment xxx; another secant has near segment 444 and far segment 888. Find xxx.
Solution
Power of a Point (secant–secant): (near1_11​)(whole1_11​) === (near2_22​)(whole2_22​):
3(3+x)=4(4+8)3(3+x)=4(4+8)3(3+x)=4(4+8)
9+3x=4(12)=489+3x=4(12)=489+3x=4(12)=48
3x=39⇒x=133x=39 \Rightarrow x=133x=39⇒x=13.
Answer: x=13x=13x=13 (whole length of the first secant =3+13=16=3+13=16=3+13=16).
Example 11: Application — line of sight to a pond's edge
An observer stands 555 km from the center of a circular pond of radius 333 km. Find the length of the tangent line of sight from the observer to the edge of the pond.
Solution
The tangent segment, the radius to the point of tangency, and the segment from the observer to the center form a right triangle (radius ⊥\perp⊥ tangent at the point of tangency):
t2+r2=d2t^2+r^2=d^2t2+r2=d2, where ddd is the distance from the observer to the center.
t2+32=52⇒t2=25−9=16⇒t=4t^2+3^2=5^2 \Rightarrow t^2=25-9=16 \Rightarrow t=4t2+32=52⇒t2=25−9=16⇒t=4.
Answer: 444 km.
