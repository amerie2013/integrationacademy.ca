10.4 Cross-Sections
A cross-section is the 2D shape formed when a plane slices through a 3D solid. The shape you get depends entirely on the orientation of the cutting plane: parallel to a base, through an axis, through an apex, tilted, or through several faces at once. Beyond identifying the shape, this worksheet computes actual cross-section dimensions and areas — for a cone or pyramid cut parallel to the base, the cross-section is similar to the base, scaled by the ratio of distances from the apex (a direct application of similar triangles); for a sphere cut off-center, the Pythagorean Theorem relates the sphere's radius, the distance to the center, and the smaller circle's radius.

Examples

Example 1: Cylinder sliced parallel to its base
A cylinder is sliced by a plane parallel to its circular bases. Describe and justify the cross-section.
Solution
Every point on the cylinder's curved surface is the same distance rrr from the central axis. A plane parallel to the bases intersects the curved surface in a set of points all still that same distance rrr from the axis — that is a circle of radius rrr, congruent to the base.
Answer: a circle congruent to the base.
Example 2: Cylinder sliced through its axis
A cylinder has radius 444 and height 999. It is sliced by a plane through its central axis, perpendicular to the bases. Describe the cross-section and find its area.
Solution
The axial cross-section of a cylinder is a rectangle: width = diameter, height = cylinder's height.
The cut passes through the full width of the circle (a diameter, 2r2r2r) and runs the full height hhh — this is a rectangle with dimensions 2r×h2r\times h2r×h.
2r=2(4)=82r=2(4)=82r=2(4)=8; height =9=9=9.
Area =8×9=72=8\times9=72=8×9=72.
Answer: rectangle, 8×98\times98×9, area 727272 square units.
Example 3: Cone sliced parallel to its base — similar triangles
A cone has base radius 888 and height 121212. A plane parallel to the base cuts the cone at a point 999 units from the apex (measured along the axis). Find the radius of the circular cross-section and its area.
Solution
Slicing parallel to the base at distance ddd from the apex gives a circle scaled by dH\dfrac{d}{H}Hd​.
The smaller cone (apex to cut) is similar to the whole cone, scaled by dH=912=34\dfrac{d}{H}=\dfrac{9}{12}=\dfrac34Hd​=129​=43​.
Cross-section radius: r′=R⋅dH=8(34)=6r'=R\cdot\dfrac{d}{H}=8\left(\dfrac34\right)=6r′=R⋅Hd​=8(43​)=6.
Area =πr′2=π(36)=36π=\pi r'^{2}=\pi(36)=36\pi=πr′2=π(36)=36π.
Answer: a circle of radius 666; area 36π36\pi36π.
Example 4: The same cone sliced through the apex and axis
Using the same cone (radius 888, height 121212) from Example 3, now slice through the apex and the axis instead. Describe the cross-section and find its area.
Solution
The axial cross-section of a cone is an isosceles triangle: base = diameter, height = the cone's height.
This cut produces an isosceles triangle with base =2R=16=2R=16=2R=16 and height =H=12=H=12=H=12 (the two equal sides run from the apex to each end of the base diameter).
Area =12(base)(height)=12(16)(12)=96=\dfrac12(\text{base})(\text{height})=\dfrac12(16)(12)=96=21​(base)(height)=21​(16)(12)=96.
Answer: isosceles triangle, base 161616, height 121212, area 969696 square units. Compare to Example 3 — the same cone gives very different cross-sections depending on the cut.
Example 5: Sphere sliced through its center
A sphere has radius 777. It is sliced by a plane through its center. Describe the cross-section and find its area.
Solution
A slice through the center is the largest possible circular cross-section: a great circle.
A cut through the center produces the largest possible circle on the sphere, called a great circle, with the same radius as the sphere: r=7r=7r=7.
Area =πr2=49π=\pi r^{2}=49\pi=πr2=49π.
Answer: a great circle of radius 777; area 49π49\pi49π.
Example 6: Sphere sliced off-center — Pythagorean Theorem
A sphere has radius 171717. It is sliced by a plane 888 units from the center (not through the center). Find the radius of the circular cross-section and its area.
Solution
Let rrr be the cross-section's radius. The sphere's radius RRR, the distance ddd from center to plane, and rrr form a right triangle (a radius to the circle's edge, the perpendicular from center to plane, and the segment on the plane):
R2=d2+r2  ⇒  172=82+r2  ⇒  289=64+r2  ⇒  r2=225  ⇒  r=15R^{2}=d^{2}+r^{2} \;\Rightarrow\; 17^{2}=8^{2}+r^{2} \;\Rightarrow\; 289=64+r^{2} \;\Rightarrow\; r^{2}=225 \;\Rightarrow\; r=15R2=d2+r2⇒172=82+r2⇒289=64+r2⇒r2=225⇒r=15.
(This is the 888-151515-171717 Pythagorean triple.)
Area =π(15)2=225π=\pi(15)^{2}=225\pi=π(15)2=225π.
Answer: radius 151515; area 225π225\pi225π — smaller than the great circle, as expected.
Example 7: Square pyramid sliced parallel to its base
A square pyramid has base edge 888 and height 121212. A plane parallel to the base cuts it 999 units from the apex. Find the edge length of the cross-section and its area.
Solution
Just like the cone, the smaller pyramid (apex to cut) is similar to the whole pyramid, scaled by 912=34\dfrac{9}{12}=\dfrac34129​=43​.
Cross-section edge: s′=8(34)=6s'=8\left(\dfrac34\right)=6s′=8(43​)=6.
Area (a square) =62=36=6^{2}=36=62=36.
Answer: a square of side 666; area 363636 square units.
Example 8: Slicing off a cube's corner
A cube has edge length 666. A plane passes through the three vertices adjacent to one corner (cutting that corner off). Describe and compute the cross-section.
Solution
Cutting through the three vertices nearest a corner always produces an equilateral triangle.
Each side of the cut triangle connects two vertices that are a face-diagonal apart (they share a face of the cube), so each side has length 626\sqrt262​ — and all three sides are equal by the cube's symmetry, so the cross-section is equilateral.
Area of an equilateral triangle with side aaa: 34a2\dfrac{\sqrt3}{4}a^{2}43​​a2, with a=62a=6\sqrt2a=62​, so a2=72a^{2}=72a2=72.
Area =34(72)=183=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{4}(72)=18\sqrt3=43​​(72)=183​.
Answer: equilateral triangle, side 626\sqrt262​, area 18318\sqrt3183​ square units.
Example 9: Slicing a cube through four vertices
A cube has edge length 555. A plane passes through four vertices, cutting straight through the middle (not through any corner). Describe and compute the cross-section.
Solution
This plane cuts through two opposite vertical edges' worth of faces, producing a rectangle whose sides are one edge of the cube (555) and one face diagonal (525\sqrt252​).
Area =5×52=252=5\times5\sqrt2=25\sqrt2=5×52​=252​.
Answer: rectangle 5×525\times5\sqrt25×52​; area 25225\sqrt2252​ square units.
Example 10: Cylinder sliced by a tilted plane
A cylinder has radius 444. A plane tilted 60∘60^\circ60∘ from the horizontal (i.e., 60∘60^\circ60∘ from a plane perpendicular to the axis) slices through it without hitting either base. Describe the cross-section and estimate its dimensions.
Solution
A tilted cut (neither parallel nor perpendicular to the axis) produces an ellipse. One axis of the ellipse still equals the cylinder's diameter (the direction unaffected by the tilt) — the semi-minor axis is r=4r=4r=4. The semi-major axis stretches by the tilt: a=rcos⁡θ=4cos⁡60∘=40.5=8a=\dfrac{r}{\cos\theta}=\dfrac{4}{\cos60^\circ}=\dfrac{4}{0.5}=8a=cosθr​=cos60∘4​=0.54​=8.
Answer: an ellipse with semi-minor axis 444 and semi-major axis 888.
