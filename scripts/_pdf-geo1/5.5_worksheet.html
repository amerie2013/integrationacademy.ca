5.5 Similarity Transformations
Two figures are similar exactly when a sequence of rigid motions (translations, rotations, reflections — which preserve size and shape) and a single dilation (which scales size by kkk but preserves shape) maps one onto the other. Rigid motions preserve every length and angle; a dilation preserves every angle but multiplies every length by ∣k∣|k|∣k∣. Because of this, a similarity transformation always preserves angle measure, and scales all lengths by the same overall factor — even a compound sequence of several dilations has a single net scale factor equal to the product of the individual factors. When k=1k=1k=1, a similarity transformation is simply a congruence transformation. Reflections are the one type of rigid motion that reverses orientation (clockwise ↔\leftrightarrow↔ counterclockwise), while translations and rotations preserve it. This worksheet practises identifying, composing, and reversing similarity transformations on the coordinate plane.

Examples

Example 1: Dilation followed by translation
△ABC\triangle ABC△ABC has A(1,1)A(1,1)A(1,1), B(3,1)B(3,1)B(3,1), C(1,4)C(1,4)C(1,4). It is dilated by k=2k=2k=2 about the origin, then translated by (5,−1)(5,-1)(5,−1). Find the final image, and explain why it is similar to △ABC\triangle ABC△ABC.
Solution
Pre-image before dilation and translation.
Dilate each vertex by k=2k=2k=2 about the origin: A′(2,2)A'(2,2)A′(2,2), B′(6,2)B'(6,2)B′(6,2), C′(2,8)C'(2,8)C′(2,8).
Translate by (5,−1)(5,-1)(5,−1): A′′(7,1)A''(7,1)A′′(7,1), B′′(11,1)B''(11,1)B′′(11,1), C′′(7,7)C''(7,7)C′′(7,7).
The dilation scales every side by k=2k=2k=2 while preserving angles; the translation is a rigid motion that changes neither size nor shape.
Conclusion: the final triangle has all angles congruent to △ABC\triangle ABC△ABC and all sides exactly twice as long — it is similar to △ABC\triangle ABC△ABC with scale factor 222.
Example 2: Describe the transformation sequence
△DEF\triangle DEF△DEF has D(0,0)D(0,0)D(0,0), E(2,0)E(2,0)E(2,0), F(0,3)F(0,3)F(0,3). It maps to D′(4,6)D'(4,6)D′(4,6), E′(8,6)E'(8,6)E′(8,6), F′(4,12)F'(4,12)F′(4,12). Describe a similarity transformation that accomplishes this.

Example 3: Scale factor from perimeter
A figure with perimeter 242424 maps under a similarity transformation to a figure with perimeter 606060. Find kkk. If a side of the original figure is 777, find the corresponding image side.
Solution
Perimeters of similar figures scale by the same factor as their sides: k=6024=52k=\dfrac{60}{24}=\dfrac{5}{2}k=2460​=25​.
Apply kkk to the given side: image side =7×52=17.5=7\times\dfrac{5}{2}=17.5=7×25​=17.5.
Answer: k=52k=\dfrac{5}{2}k=25​; corresponding image side =17.5=17.5=17.5.
Example 4: Reflection reverses orientation
△PQR\triangle PQR△PQR has P(0,0)P(0,0)P(0,0), Q(4,0)Q(4,0)Q(4,0), R(0,3)R(0,3)R(0,3), listed counterclockwise. It is reflected over the yyy-axis. Find the image vertices and determine the new orientation.
Solution
Reflecting over the yyy-axis negates every xxx-coordinate.
Apply the reflection rule (x,y)↦(−x,y)(x,y)\mapsto(-x,y)(x,y)↦(−x,y): P′(0,0)P'(0,0)P′(0,0), Q′(−4,0)Q'(-4,0)Q′(−4,0), R′(0,3)R'(0,3)R′(0,3).
In the original, tracing P→Q→RP\to Q\to RP→Q→R moves right then up-left — counterclockwise.
In the image, tracing P′→Q′→R′P'\to Q'\to R'P′→Q′→R′ moves left then up-right — the direction of travel has reversed.
Conclusion: the image is traversed clockwise — reflections always reverse orientation, unlike translations or rotations.
Example 5: The congruence special case
A triangle is rotated 90∘90^{\circ}90∘ about the origin, with no dilation applied. Explain why this is a similarity transformation with k=1k=1k=1, and name the special case.
Solution
A rotation is a rigid motion — it preserves every length and angle exactly, with no scaling at all.
Since no length changes, the effective scale factor is k=1k=1k=1.
Conclusion: a similarity transformation with k=1k=1k=1 is simply a congruence transformation — congruence is the special case of similarity where nothing is resized.
Example 6: Find $k$ from a pure dilation
Point (3,5)(3,5)(3,5) maps to (9,15)(9,15)(9,15) under a dilation about the origin (no other transformations). Find kkk, confirming with both coordinates.
Solution
Using the xxx-coordinates: k=93=3k=\dfrac{9}{3}=3k=39​=3.
Check with the yyy-coordinates: k=155=3k=\dfrac{15}{5}=3k=515​=3 ✓ — consistent.
Answer: k=3k=3k=3.
Example 7: Composing two dilations
A figure is dilated about the origin by k1=2k_1=2k1​=2, and the image is then dilated about the origin again by k2=3k_2=3k2​=3. Find the net scale factor, and verify using the point (2,1)(2,1)(2,1).
Solution
The net scale factor of two dilations about the same center is the product of the individual factors: knet=k1×k2=2×3=6k_{\text{net}}=k_1\times k_2=2\times3=6knet​=k1​×k2​=2×3=6.
Apply step by step to (2,1)(2,1)(2,1): first dilation gives (4,2)(4,2)(4,2); second dilation gives (12,6)(12,6)(12,6).
Apply the net factor directly: (2,1)×6=(12,6)(2,1)\times6=(12,6)(2,1)×6=(12,6) ✓ — matches.
Answer: net scale factor =6=6=6.
Example 8: Scale factor from a rotation + dilation
A triangle has a side of length 666. After a rotation followed by a dilation, the corresponding image side measures 212121. Find the scale factor kkk used in the dilation.
Solution
A rotation alone does not change any length, so all of the length change comes from the dilation.
k=image lengthoriginal length=216=3.5k=\dfrac{\text{image length}}{\text{original length}}=\dfrac{21}{6}=3.5k=original lengthimage length​=621​=3.5.
Answer: k=3.5k=3.5k=3.5.
Example 9: Logo scaling (word problem)
A company logo triangle has a base of 888 cm. A larger version for a billboard is created using a similarity transformation with k=4.5k=4.5k=4.5. Find the billboard version's base length.
Solution
Apply the scale factor directly to the given length:
billboard base =8×4.5=36=8\times4.5=36=8×4.5=36 cm.
Answer: 363636 cm.
Example 10: Angle measure is preserved
△XYZ\triangle XYZ△XYZ has ∠X=65∘\angle X=65^{\circ}∠X=65∘. It is reflected over the xxx-axis, then dilated by k=25k=\dfrac{2}{5}k=52​ about the origin. Find the measure of the corresponding angle in the final image, and explain why.
Solution
A reflection is a rigid motion, so it preserves angle measure exactly.
A dilation scales every length by kkk but leaves every angle measure unchanged (it only stretches distances, not the geometry of the angles).
Since neither transformation in the sequence changes angle measure, the final angle is unchanged.
Answer: 65∘65^{\circ}65∘ — similarity transformations always preserve angle measure, even when they scale lengths.
