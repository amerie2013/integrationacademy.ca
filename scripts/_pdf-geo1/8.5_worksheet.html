8.5 Dilations: Perimeter and Area
A dilation with scale factor kkk centered at the origin sends point (x,y)(x,y)(x,y) to (kx,ky)(kx,ky)(kx,ky) — every length in the figure is multiplied by ∣k∣|k|∣k∣. Since perimeter is a sum of lengths, it scales the same way: P′=∣k∣⋅PP'=|k|\cdot PP′=∣k∣⋅P. Area, however, is a product of two lengths (like base times height), so it scales by k2k^2k2: A′=k2⋅AA'=k^2\cdot AA′=k2⋅A. This squared relationship also works in reverse — if you know how areas compare, take a square root to find how sides or perimeters compare, and vice versa. This worksheet includes coordinate dilations and real-world scale-model problems that combine both relationships.

Examples

Example 1: Perimeter scales by $k$
A figure has perimeter 202020. It is dilated with scale factor k=3k=3k=3. Find the image's perimeter.
Solution
Every length in the image is kkk times the corresponding length in the original.
Identify the relationship: perimeter is a sum of side lengths, and every length scales by the factor kkk under a dilation, so P′=k⋅PP'=k\cdot PP′=k⋅P.
Compute: P′=3(20)=60P'=3(20)=60P′=3(20)=60.
Answer: 606060.
Example 2: Area scales by $k^2$
A figure has area 121212. It is dilated with scale factor k=2k=2k=2. Find the image's area.
Solution
Identify the relationship: area is a product of two lengths (like base ×\times× height), and each of those lengths scales by kkk, so the area scales by k2k^2k2: A′=k2⋅AA'=k^2\cdot AA′=k2⋅A.
Compute: A′=22(12)=4(12)=48A'=2^2(12)=4(12)=48A′=22(12)=4(12)=48.
Answer: 484848.
Example 3: Find $k$ from a perimeter change
A perimeter maps from 181818 to 454545 under a dilation. Find kkk.
Solution
Identify: since perimeter scales linearly (P′=k⋅PP'=k\cdot PP′=k⋅P), the scale factor is the ratio of the new perimeter to the old one.
Compute: k=P′P=4518=52k=\dfrac{P'}{P}=\dfrac{45}{18}=\dfrac{5}{2}k=PP′​=1845​=25​.
Answer: k=52k=\dfrac{5}{2}k=25​ (or 2.52.52.5).
Example 4: Area with a fractional scale factor
A figure has area 404040. It is dilated with k=12k=\dfrac{1}{2}k=21​. Find the image's area.
Solution
Identify the rule: area scales by k2k^2k2, regardless of whether kkk is a whole number or a fraction.
Compute: A′=(12)2(40)=14(40)=10A'=\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^2(40)=\dfrac{1}{4}(40)=10A′=(21​)2(40)=41​(40)=10.
Answer: 101010. Note a fractional k<1k<1k<1 shrinks the figure, so the smaller area makes sense.
Example 5: Side ratio to area ratio
Two similar figures have corresponding sides 666 and 999. Find the ratio of their areas (smaller to larger).
Solution
Identify the scale factor from the given sides: k=96=32k=\dfrac{9}{6}=\dfrac{3}{2}k=69​=23​.
Compute the area ratio by squaring the scale factor: k2=(32)2=94k^2=\left(\dfrac{3}{2}\right)^2=\dfrac{9}{4}k2=(23​)2=49​.
Answer: 94\dfrac{9}{4}49​ (or 9:49:49:4).
Example 6: Area ratio to perimeter ratio
Two similar figures have areas in ratio 4:14:14:1. Find the ratio of their perimeters.
Solution
Perimeter (and side) ratio is the square root of the area ratio:
4:1=2:1\sqrt{4}:\sqrt{1}=2:14​:1​=2:1.
Answer: 2:12:12:1.
Example 7: Reduction (shrinking)
A figure has perimeter 505050. It is dilated with k=0.4k=0.4k=0.4. Find the image's perimeter.
Solution
Identify: a scale factor less than 111 shrinks the figure, but the rule P′=k⋅PP'=k\cdot PP′=k⋅P still applies directly.
Compute: P′=0.4(50)=20P'=0.4(50)=20P′=0.4(50)=20.
Answer: 202020, which is smaller than the original, as expected since k<1k<1k<1.
Example 8: Square — check both rules at once
A square has side 555. It is dilated with k=3k=3k=3. Find the image's perimeter and area two ways each.
Solution
Perimeter: original =4(5)=20=4(5)=20=4(5)=20; using the scale rule, image =3(20)=60=3(20)=60=3(20)=60. Directly: new side =3(5)=15=3(5)=15=3(5)=15, so image perimeter =4(15)=60=4(15)=60=4(15)=60. ✓
Area: original =52=25=5^2=25=52=25; using the scale rule, image =32(25)=225=3^2(25)=225=32(25)=225. Directly: new side =15=15=15, so image area =152=225=15^2=225=152=225. ✓
Example 9: Find the original area
After a dilation with k=3k=3k=3, a figure's image has area 727272. Find the original area.
Solution
Identify: this reverses the usual direction — solve A′=k2AA'=k^2AA′=k2A for the original area AAA by dividing instead of multiplying.
Compute: A=A′k2=7232=729=8A=\dfrac{A'}{k^2}=\dfrac{72}{3^2}=\dfrac{72}{9}=8A=k2A′​=3272​=972​=8.
Answer: 888.
Example 10: Coordinate dilation — verify both rules
A triangle has vertices (0,0)(0,0)(0,0), (4,0)(4,0)(4,0), (0,3)(0,3)(0,3). Dilate it from the origin with k=3k=3k=3. Find the new vertices, and verify that the perimeter scaled by kkk and the area scaled by k2k^2k2.
Solution
New vertices: multiply each coordinate by 333: (0,0)(0,0)(0,0), (12,0)(12,0)(12,0), (0,9)(0,9)(0,9).
Original: legs 444, 333; hypotenuse 16+9=5\sqrt{16+9}=516+9​=5. Perimeter =4+3+5=12=4+3+5=12=4+3+5=12. Area =12(4)(3)=6=\dfrac{1}{2}(4)(3)=6=21​(4)(3)=6.
Image: legs 121212, 999; hypotenuse 144+81=15\sqrt{144+81}=15144+81​=15. Perimeter =12+9+15=36=12+9+15=36=12+9+15=36. Area =12(12)(9)=54=\dfrac{1}{2}(12)(9)=54=21​(12)(9)=54.
Check: 36=3(12)36=3(12)36=3(12) ✓ (perimeter ×k\times k×k); 54=9(6)=32(6)54=9(6)=3^2(6)54=9(6)=32(6) ✓ (area ×k2\times k^2×k2).
Example 11: Application — scale model of a house
An architect's model house has a floor perimeter of 888 ft and a floor area of 333 ft2^22. The real house is built at scale factor 505050 (every model length corresponds to 505050 times that length in real life). Find the real house's floor perimeter and floor area.
Solution
Perimeter: P′=50(8)=400P'=50(8)=400P′=50(8)=400 ft.
Area: A′=502(3)=2500(3)=7500A'=50^2(3)=2500(3)=7500A′=502(3)=2500(3)=7500 ft2^22.
Answer: real perimeter =400=400=400 ft; real area =7500=7500=7500 ft2^22.
