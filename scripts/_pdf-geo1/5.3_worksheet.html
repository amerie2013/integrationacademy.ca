5.3 Indirect Measurement
Similar triangles let us find heights and distances that are impractical to measure directly. In the shadow method, the sun's rays are essentially parallel, so an object and its shadow form a right triangle similar to any other object-and-shadow triangle measured at the same moment (AA: both right angles, plus the shared angle of elevation to the sun). In the mirror method, the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection at a mirror on the ground, creating two similar right triangles. Surveyors use the same idea to measure distances across rivers or ponds using sightlines and stakes. In every case, the key step is setting up a correct proportion that matches height-to-height and base-to-base (never height-to-base). Some problems add an extra step, such as an observer's eye height, after the proportion is solved. This worksheet practises all of these indirect-measurement setups with full multi-step solutions.

Examples

Example 1: Shadow method
A 666 ft person casts a 444 ft shadow. At the same moment, a tree casts a 202020 ft shadow. Find the tree's height.
Solution
Both the person and the tree form similar right triangles with their shadows and the sun's rays.
Identify similar triangles: the person and the tree each form a right triangle with the ground and the sun's rays; both have a right angle and share the same angle of elevation to the sun (parallel rays) ⇒\Rightarrow⇒ similar by AA.
Set up the proportion (height over shadow, matched):
64=h20\dfrac{6}{4}=\dfrac{h}{20}46​=20h​
Cross-multiply: 4h=1204h=1204h=120
h=30h=30h=30.
Answer: the tree is 303030 ft tall.
Example 2: Mirror method
A mirror lies flat on the ground 888 ft from a student and 242424 ft from a building. The student's eyes are 555 ft above the ground, and she sees the top of the building reflected in the mirror. Find the building's height.
Solution
Identify similar triangles: at the mirror, the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection, so the student's sightline triangle and the building's triangle share a congruent angle at the mirror; both also have a right angle (heights are vertical) ⇒\Rightarrow⇒ similar by AA.
Set up the proportion (height over distance to mirror):
58=h24\dfrac{5}{8}=\dfrac{h}{24}85​=24h​
Cross-multiply: 8h=1208h=1208h=120
h=15h=15h=15.
Answer: the building is 151515 ft tall.

Example 4: Ramp — height at a given point
A skateboard ramp rises 333 ft over a horizontal run of 121212 ft. How high is the ramp surface at a point 888 ft along the horizontal from the base?
Solution
The small right triangle up to the 888 ft mark is similar to the full ramp triangle (same base angle, both right triangles).
Set up the proportion (rise over run):
312=h8\dfrac{3}{12}=\dfrac{h}{8}123​=8h​
h=3×812=2h=\dfrac{3\times8}{12}=2h=123×8​=2.
Answer: the ramp is 222 ft high at that point.
Example 5: Surveying across a river
A surveyor sets up similar triangles △ABE∼△CDE\triangle ABE \sim \triangle CDE△ABE∼△CDE on opposite sides of a river to find its width ABABAB. On the near bank she measures AE=40AE=40AE=40, CE=10CE=10CE=10, and CD=15CD=15CD=15. Find the river width ABABAB.
Solution
Sightlines from the stakes create two similar triangles across the river.
Since △ABE∼△CDE\triangle ABE \sim \triangle CDE△ABE∼△CDE, corresponding sides are proportional:
ABCD=AECE\dfrac{AB}{CD}=\dfrac{AE}{CE}CDAB​=CEAE​
AB15=4010=4\dfrac{AB}{15}=\dfrac{40}{10}=415AB​=1040​=4
AB=4×15=60AB=4\times15=60AB=4×15=60.
Answer: the river is 606060 units wide.
Example 6: Thales' method with decimals
A 1.51.51.5 m stick held vertically casts a 0.50.50.5 m shadow. At the same time, a tower casts a 121212 m shadow. Find the tower's height.
Solution
The stick and the tower cast shadows at the same angle of elevation to the sun.
Set up the proportion:
1.50.5=h12\dfrac{1.5}{0.5}=\dfrac{h}{12}0.51.5​=12h​
1.50.5=3\dfrac{1.5}{0.5}=30.51.5​=3, so h=3×12=36h=3\times12=36h=3×12=36.
Answer: the tower is 363636 m tall.
Example 7: Find the missing shadow
A 999 ft statue casts a shadow at the same time a 666 ft person casts a 444 ft shadow. Find the length of the statue's shadow.
Solution
Set up the proportion (height over shadow, same for both):
64=9s\dfrac{6}{4}=\dfrac{9}{s}46​=s9​
Cross-multiply: 6s=366s=366s=36
s=6s=6s=6.
Answer: the statue's shadow is 666 ft long.
Example 8: Scale drawing (map)
On a map, 222 cm represents 505050 m of actual distance. A hiking path measures 777 cm on the map. Find the actual length of the path in meters, then convert to kilometers.
Solution
Set up the proportion (map over actual, consistent units):
250=7L\dfrac{2}{50}=\dfrac{7}{L}502​=L7​
Cross-multiply: 2L=3502L=3502L=350
L=175L=175L=175 m.
Convert: 175 m=0.175175\text{ m}=0.175175 m=0.175 km.
Answer: 175175175 m, or 0.1750.1750.175 km.
Example 9: Clinometer — a compound problem
A student's eyes are 555 ft above the ground, standing 303030 ft from a flagpole. Using a clinometer, the line of sight to the top forms a right triangle similar to a reference triangle with legs 444 (rise) and 555 (run). Find the flagpole's total height.
Solution
Find the rise from eye level to the top of the pole using the similar triangles:
x30=45\dfrac{x}{30}=\dfrac{4}{5}30x​=54​
x=4×305=24x=\dfrac{4\times30}{5}=24x=54×30​=24 ft (this is the height above eye level).
Add the eye height, since the clinometer sightline starts 555 ft off the ground, not at ground level:
Total height =24+5=29=24+5=29=24+5=29 ft.
Answer: the flagpole is 292929 ft tall.
