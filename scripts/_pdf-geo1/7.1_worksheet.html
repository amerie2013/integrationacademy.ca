7.1 Polygon Angle Sums
Every convex polygon's interior angles can be found by splitting the polygon into triangles from one vertex: an nnn-gon splits into (n−2)(n-2)(n−2) triangles, and since each triangle contributes 180∘180^\circ180∘, the interior angle sum is S=(n−2)⋅180∘S=(n-2)\cdot180^\circS=(n−2)⋅180∘. Exterior angles (one per vertex, formed by extending each side) always add to exactly 360∘360^\circ360∘ for any convex polygon, no matter how many sides it has — walking once around the perimeter turns you through one full rotation. For a regular polygon (congruent sides and angles), split these totals evenly: each interior angle is (n−2)180∘n\dfrac{(n-2)180^\circ}{n}n(n−2)180∘​, and each exterior angle is 360∘n\dfrac{360^\circ}{n}n360∘​. At any single vertex, the interior and exterior angles are supplementary. This worksheet goes beyond plugging into a formula — expect triangulation reasoning, two-variable algebra, and word problems that combine both formulas.

Examples

Example 1: Triangulate a hexagon (see why the formula works)
Find the sum of the interior angles of a convex hexagon by triangulating it from one vertex, then confirm with the formula.
Solution
A hexagon splits into 6−2=46-2=46−2=4 triangles when diagonals are drawn from one vertex.
Draw diagonals from one vertex to every non-adjacent vertex. This splits the hexagon into 6−2=46-2=46−2=4 triangles.
Each triangle's angles sum to 180∘180^\circ180∘, and together the triangles' angles make up exactly the hexagon's interior angles, so:
S=4⋅180∘=720∘S = 4\cdot180^\circ = 720^\circS=4⋅180∘=720∘.
Check with the formula: S=(n−2)180∘=(6−2)180∘=720∘S=(n-2)180^\circ=(6-2)180^\circ=720^\circS=(n−2)180∘=(6−2)180∘=720∘. ✓
Answer: 720∘720^\circ720∘.
Example 2: Regular octagon — two methods
A stop sign is a regular octagon. Find the measure of each interior angle two different ways.
Solution
A regular octagon: all 8 sides and all 8 interior angles are congruent.
Method 1 (interior sum): S=(8−2)180∘=1080∘S=(8-2)180^\circ=1080^\circS=(8−2)180∘=1080∘. Divide evenly among the 888 congruent angles: 1080∘8=135∘\dfrac{1080^\circ}{8}=135^\circ81080∘​=135∘.
Method 2 (exterior angle): Each exterior angle is 360∘8=45∘\dfrac{360^\circ}{8}=45^\circ8360∘​=45∘. Since interior and exterior angles at a vertex are supplementary, interior =180∘−45∘=135∘=180^\circ-45^\circ=135^\circ=180∘−45∘=135∘.
Both methods agree.
Answer: 135∘135^\circ135∘.
Example 3: Find $n$ from the interior sum
The interior angles of a convex polygon sum to 1440∘1440^\circ1440∘. How many sides does it have?
Solution
Set up the formula and solve for nnn:
(n−2)180∘=1440∘(n-2)180^\circ=1440^\circ(n−2)180∘=1440∘
n−2=1440180=8n-2=\dfrac{1440}{180}=8n−2=1801440​=8
n=10n=10n=10.
Check: (10−2)180∘=8⋅180∘=1440∘(10-2)180^\circ=8\cdot180^\circ=1440^\circ(10−2)180∘=8⋅180∘=1440∘. ✓
Answer: a decagon (101010 sides).
Example 4: Regular $15$-gon — exterior then interior
A regular polygon has 151515 sides. Find each exterior angle, then each interior angle, and verify your interior angle with the sum formula.
Solution
Exterior angle: 360∘15=24∘\dfrac{360^\circ}{15}=24^\circ15360∘​=24∘.
Interior angle (supplementary to exterior): 180∘−24∘=156∘180^\circ-24^\circ=156^\circ180∘−24∘=156∘.
Verify: interior sum should be (15−2)180∘=2340∘(15-2)180^\circ=2340^\circ(15−2)180∘=2340∘; and 15⋅156∘=2340∘15\cdot156^\circ=2340^\circ15⋅156∘=2340∘. ✓
Answer: exterior =24∘=24^\circ=24∘, interior =156∘=156^\circ=156∘.
Example 5: Missing angle in an irregular pentagon
A pentagon has angles 100∘100^\circ100∘, 110∘110^\circ110∘, 95∘95^\circ95∘, 120∘120^\circ120∘, and xxx. Find xxx.
Solution
Interior sum for a pentagon: S=(5−2)180∘=540∘S=(5-2)180^\circ=540^\circS=(5−2)180∘=540∘.
Add the known angles: 100+110+95+120=425∘100+110+95+120=425^\circ100+110+95+120=425∘.
x=540∘−425∘=115∘x=540^\circ-425^\circ=115^\circx=540∘−425∘=115∘.
Answer: x=115∘x=115^\circx=115∘.
Example 6: Does the exterior-sum rule hold for a triangle?
Verify that the exterior angles of an equilateral triangle sum to 360∘360^\circ360∘, just like any other convex polygon.
Solution
An equilateral triangle has three congruent interior angles of 60∘60^\circ60∘ each.
Each exterior angle is supplementary to its interior angle: 180∘−60∘=120∘180^\circ-60^\circ=120^\circ180∘−60∘=120∘.
Sum of the three exterior angles: 3⋅120∘=360∘3\cdot120^\circ=360^\circ3⋅120∘=360∘.
So the exterior-angle-sum rule (360∘360^\circ360∘) holds true even for the smallest possible polygon, n=3n=3n=3. This rule never depends on nnn.
Example 7: Find $n$ from an exterior angle
Each exterior angle of a regular polygon measures 40∘40^\circ40∘. How many sides does the polygon have?
Solution
The exterior angles of a regular nnn-gon are all equal and sum to 360∘360^\circ360∘, so each one is 360∘n\dfrac{360^\circ}{n}n360∘​.
40∘=360∘n⇒n=360∘40∘=940^\circ=\dfrac{360^\circ}{n} \Rightarrow n=\dfrac{360^\circ}{40^\circ}=940∘=n360∘​⇒n=40∘360∘​=9.
Answer: a regular nonagon (999 sides).
Example 8: Algebraic quadrilateral
A quadrilateral has angles 3x3x3x, 2x2x2x, 4x4x4x, and xxx. Find xxx and each angle, then check the sum.
Solution
Interior sum of a quadrilateral: (4−2)180∘=360∘(4-2)180^\circ=360^\circ(4−2)180∘=360∘.
3x+2x+4x+x=360∘⇒10x=360∘⇒x=36∘3x+2x+4x+x=360^\circ \Rightarrow 10x=360^\circ \Rightarrow x=36^\circ3x+2x+4x+x=360∘⇒10x=360∘⇒x=36∘.
Angles: 3x=108∘3x=108^\circ3x=108∘, 2x=72∘2x=72^\circ2x=72∘, 4x=144∘4x=144^\circ4x=144∘, x=36∘x=36^\circx=36∘.
Check: 108+72+144+36=360∘108+72+144+36=360^\circ108+72+144+36=360∘. ✓
Example 9: Nonagon interior sum
Find the interior angle sum of a convex nonagon (999 sides), and the measure of each angle if it is regular.
Solution
S=(9−2)180∘=7⋅180∘=1260∘S=(9-2)180^\circ=7\cdot180^\circ=1260^\circS=(9−2)180∘=7⋅180∘=1260∘.
If regular, each angle =1260∘9=140∘=\dfrac{1260^\circ}{9}=140^\circ=91260∘​=140∘.
Answer: sum =1260∘=1260^\circ=1260∘; each angle (if regular) =140∘=140^\circ=140∘.
Example 10: Interior sum as a multiple of the exterior sum
A convex polygon's interior angle sum is exactly 333 times its exterior angle sum. Find the number of sides.
Solution
The exterior sum is always 360∘360^\circ360∘ for a convex polygon, so the interior sum here is 3⋅360∘=1080∘3\cdot360^\circ=1080^\circ3⋅360∘=1080∘.
(n−2)180∘=1080∘(n-2)180^\circ=1080^\circ(n−2)180∘=1080∘
n−2=6n-2=6n−2=6
n=8n=8n=8.
Answer: 888 sides (an octagon).
Example 11: Can regular pentagons tile a floor?
A contractor wants to tile a floor edge-to-edge using only regular pentagon tiles, with tiles meeting perfectly at every point (angles around a point must add to exactly 360∘360^\circ360∘). Determine whether this is possible.
Solution
Each interior angle of a regular pentagon: (5−2)180∘5=540∘5=108∘\dfrac{(5-2)180^\circ}{5}=\dfrac{540^\circ}{5}=108^\circ5(5−2)180∘​=5540∘​=108∘.
For tiles to meet perfectly at a point with no gaps or overlaps, 108∘108^\circ108∘ must divide evenly into 360∘360^\circ360∘:
360∘108∘=3.3‾\dfrac{360^\circ}{108^\circ}=3.\overline{3}108∘360∘​=3.3 — not a whole number.
Answer: No. Three pentagons leave a 360−3(108)=36∘360-3(108)=36^\circ360−3(108)=36∘ gap, and a fourth would overlap. (This is why floor and wall tiles are usually squares, equilateral triangles, or regular hexagons — the only regular polygons whose interior angle divides evenly into 360∘360^\circ360∘.)
