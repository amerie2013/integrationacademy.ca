6.5 Angles of Elevation and Depression
The angle of elevation is measured upward from a horizontal line of sight to an object above; the angle of depression is measured downward from horizontal to an object below. Because the horizontal lines at the observer and at the object are parallel, and the line of sight is a transversal between them, the angle of elevation from the lower point equals the angle of depression from the higher point — they are alternate interior angles. Every elevation/depression problem becomes a right triangle: the horizontal distance is one leg, the height difference is the other leg, and tan⁡θ=heightdistance\tan\theta=\dfrac{\text{height}}{\text{distance}}tanθ=distanceheight​ connects them (with sin⁡\sinsin or cos⁡\coscos used when the hypotenuse — the actual line of sight — is involved instead). Some problems require an extra step, such as adding an observer's eye height, or subtracting two elevation readings to isolate the height of just part of an object (like a window). This worksheet models real situations with full multi-step right-triangle trigonometry.

Examples

Example 1: Basic elevation
From a point 404040 ft from the base of a building, the angle of elevation to the top is 30∘30^{\circ}30∘. Find the building's height.
Solution
Horizontal distance =40=40=40; angle of elevation =30∘=30^{\circ}=30∘; height is the unknown leg.
Identify the right triangle: horizontal distance =40=40=40 (adjacent to θ\thetaθ), height =h=h=h (opposite).
Apply TOA: tan⁡30∘=h40\tan 30^{\circ}=\dfrac{h}{40}tan30∘=40h​
Solve: h=40tan⁡30∘=40⋅13=4033h=40\tan 30^{\circ}=40\cdot\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}=\dfrac{40\sqrt{3}}{3}h=40tan30∘=40⋅3​1​=3403​​ ft.
Answer: 4033\dfrac{40\sqrt{3}}{3}3403​​ ft.
Example 2: Depression to find distance
From the top of a 505050 m cliff, the angle of depression to a boat is 25∘25^{\circ}25∘. Find the horizontal distance to the boat.
Solution
The angle of depression from the cliff top equals the angle of elevation from the boat (alternate interior angles, parallel horizontals) =25∘=25^{\circ}=25∘.
In the right triangle: opposite (cliff height) =50=50=50, adjacent (distance) =d=d=d.
Apply TOA: tan⁡25∘=50d\tan 25^{\circ}=\dfrac{50}{d}tan25∘=d50​
Solve: d=50tan⁡25∘d=\dfrac{50}{\tan 25^{\circ}}d=tan25∘50​ m.
Answer: d=50tan⁡25∘d=\dfrac{50}{\tan 25^{\circ}}d=tan25∘50​ m.
Example 3: Elevation of $45^{\circ}$
From 202020 m away, the angle of elevation to the top of a pole is 45∘45^{\circ}45∘. Find the height.
Solution
Identify the right triangle: horizontal distance =20=20=20 (adjacent to θ\thetaθ), height =h=h=h (opposite).
Apply TOA: tan⁡45∘=h20\tan 45^{\circ}=\dfrac{h}{20}tan45∘=20h​
Since tan⁡45∘=1\tan 45^{\circ}=1tan45∘=1: h=20×1=20h=20\times1=20h=20×1=20 m.
Answer: 202020 m (at 45∘45^{\circ}45∘, height always equals distance).
Example 4: Prove the equal-angles relationship
Prove that the angle of elevation from a ground point AAA to a point BBB above equals the angle of depression from BBB back to AAA.
Solution
The horizontal line at AAA and the horizontal line at BBB are both horizontal, so they are parallel.
Segment AB‾\overline{AB}AB (the line of sight) is a transversal cutting these two parallel horizontal lines.
The angle of elevation at AAA (above the horizontal at AAA) and the angle of depression at BBB (below the horizontal at BBB) are alternate interior angles formed by this transversal.
Conclusion: alternate interior angles of parallel lines are congruent, so the angle of elevation from AAA equals the angle of depression from BBB.
Example 5: Tree height at $60^{\circ}$
From 151515 ft away, the angle of elevation to a tree's top is 60∘60^{\circ}60∘. Find the height.
Solution
Identify the right triangle: horizontal distance =15=15=15 (adjacent to θ\thetaθ), height =h=h=h (opposite).
Apply TOA: tan⁡60∘=h15\tan 60^{\circ}=\dfrac{h}{15}tan60∘=15h​
h=15tan⁡60∘=153h=15\tan 60^{\circ}=15\sqrt{3}h=15tan60∘=153​ ft.
Answer: 15315\sqrt{3}153​ ft.
Example 6: Find the angle from a ramp
A ramp rises 444 ft over a horizontal run of 121212 ft. Find the angle of elevation.
Solution
Identify the legs of the right triangle: rise (opposite) =4=4=4, run (adjacent) =12=12=12.
Apply TOA: tan⁡θ=412=13\tan\theta=\dfrac{4}{12}=\dfrac{1}{3}tanθ=124​=31​
Solve with the inverse tangent, since the angle itself — not a side — is unknown:
θ=tan⁡−1 ⁣(13)\theta=\tan^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)θ=tan−1(31​).
Answer: θ=tan⁡−1 ⁣(13)\theta=\tan^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)θ=tan−1(31​).
Example 7: Depression from a lighthouse
From a lighthouse 808080 ft tall, the angle of depression to a ship is 20∘20^{\circ}20∘. Express the ship's distance from the base.
Solution
By the equal-angles relationship, the angle of elevation from the ship is also 20∘20^{\circ}20∘.
Apply TOA: tan⁡20∘=80d\tan 20^{\circ}=\dfrac{80}{d}tan20∘=d80​
Solve: d=80tan⁡20∘d=\dfrac{80}{\tan 20^{\circ}}d=tan20∘80​ ft.
Answer: d=80tan⁡20∘d=\dfrac{80}{\tan 20^{\circ}}d=tan20∘80​ ft.
Example 8: Window height (subtracting two elevations)
From 252525 m away, the angle of elevation to the top of a window is 50∘50^{\circ}50∘, and to the bottom of the window is 30∘30^{\circ}30∘. Find the window's height.
Solution
Find the height to the top: htop=25tan⁡50∘h_{\text{top}}=25\tan 50^{\circ}htop​=25tan50∘.
Find the height to the bottom: hbottom=25tan⁡30∘h_{\text{bottom}}=25\tan 30^{\circ}hbottom​=25tan30∘.
The window's height is the difference between these two heights: htop−hbottom=25tan⁡50∘−25tan⁡30∘h_{\text{top}}-h_{\text{bottom}}=25\tan 50^{\circ}-25\tan 30^{\circ}htop​−hbottom​=25tan50∘−25tan30∘.
Answer: 25(tan⁡50∘−tan⁡30∘)25(\tan 50^{\circ}-\tan 30^{\circ})25(tan50∘−tan30∘) m.
Example 9: Ladder at an angle
A ladder leans at a 70∘70^{\circ}70∘ angle of elevation from the ground, with its base 333 ft from the wall. Find how high it reaches on the wall.
Solution
The ladder is the hypotenuse; the wall height is the opposite leg.
Identify the right triangle: horizontal base distance =3=3=3 (adjacent to θ\thetaθ), height on the wall =h=h=h (opposite).
Apply TOA: tan⁡70∘=h3\tan 70^{\circ}=\dfrac{h}{3}tan70∘=3h​
h=3tan⁡70∘h=3\tan 70^{\circ}h=3tan70∘ ft.
Answer: 3tan⁡70∘3\tan 70^{\circ}3tan70∘ ft.
Example 10: Compound problem with eye height
A person whose eyes are 555 ft above the ground stands 606060 ft from a tower. The angle of elevation from her eyes to the top of the tower is 35∘35^{\circ}35∘. Find the tower's total height.
Solution
Find the rise above eye level using the right triangle from eye level: tan⁡35∘=x60\tan 35^{\circ}=\dfrac{x}{60}tan35∘=60x​
x=60tan⁡35∘x=60\tan 35^{\circ}x=60tan35∘ (height above eye level).
Add the eye height, since the sightline starts 555 ft off the ground, not at ground level: total =60tan⁡35∘+5=60\tan 35^{\circ}+5=60tan35∘+5.
Answer: (60tan⁡35∘+5)(60\tan 35^{\circ}+5)(60tan35∘+5) ft.
