4.4 ASA and AAS Congruence
The ASA Postulate proves triangles congruent from two pairs of congruent angles plus the included side (the side between those two angles). The AAS Theorem proves congruence from two pairs of congruent angles plus a non-included side — a side that lies opposite one of the given angles rather than between them. AAS actually reduces to ASA: since two angles are congruent, the Angle-Sum Theorem forces the third pair of angles to be congruent too, which makes the given side included relative to that third angle. Note that AAA is never a valid congruence criterion — matching all three angles only guarantees the triangles are the same shape (similar), not the same size. This worksheet applies ASA and AAS with numeric, algebraic, and overlapping-triangle reasoning, and contrasts them with AAA.
