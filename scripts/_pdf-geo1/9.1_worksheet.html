9.1 Circle Vocabulary & Central Angles
A circle is the set of all points a fixed distance (the radius) from a center point. A chord connects any two points on the circle; a diameter is a chord through the center and is the longest possible chord, with length 2r2r2r. A central angle has its vertex at the center, and its measure always equals the measure of the arc it intercepts — this is the single most important rule in circle geometry, since it converts angle problems into arc problems and back. Arcs smaller than a semicircle are minor arcs; arcs larger are major arcs; an arc cut off by a diameter is a semicircle (180∘180^\circ180∘). The Arc Addition Postulate works just like the Segment and Angle Addition Postulates: adjacent arcs add up. Since central angles all the way around a point sum to 360∘360^\circ360∘, so do all the arcs of a circle. This worksheet uses these rules together, including algebra and real-world angle problems.

Examples

Example 1: Minor and major arcs
Central angle ∠AOB=72∘\angle AOB=72^\circ∠AOB=72∘. Find the measures of minor arc ABABAB and major arc ABABAB.
Solution
The central angle and its intercepted (minor) arc always have equal measure.
Minor arc equals the central angle: mAB^=72∘m\widehat{AB}=72^\circmAB=72∘.
Major arc is the rest of the circle: 360∘−72∘=288∘360^\circ-72^\circ=288^\circ360∘−72∘=288∘.
Answer: minor arc =72∘=72^\circ=72∘; major arc =288∘=288^\circ=288∘.
Example 2: Arc Addition Postulate
mAB^=40∘m\widehat{AB}=40^\circmAB=40∘ and mBC^=65∘m\widehat{BC}=65^\circmBC=65∘, with BBB between AAA and CCC on the circle. Find mAC^m\widehat{AC}mAC.
Solution
Adjacent arcs add, just like adjacent angles:
mAC^=mAB^+mBC^=40∘+65∘=105∘m\widehat{AC}=m\widehat{AB}+m\widehat{BC}=40^\circ+65^\circ=105^\circmAC=mAB+mBC=40∘+65∘=105∘.
Answer: 105∘105^\circ105∘.
Example 3: Central angles around a point sum to $360^\circ$
Three central angles around a center measure 110∘110^\circ110∘, 95∘95^\circ95∘, and xxx. Find xxx.
Solution
Identify the relationship: all central angles around a point sum to 360∘360^\circ360∘.
Set up and solve the equation:
110∘+95∘+x=360∘110^\circ+95^\circ+x=360^\circ110∘+95∘+x=360∘
205∘+x=360∘205^\circ+x=360^\circ205∘+x=360∘
x=155∘x=155^\circx=155∘.
Answer: 155∘155^\circ155∘.
Example 4: Diameter forms a linear pair of central angles
AAA and BBB are endpoints of a diameter; CCC is another point on the circle. m∠AOC=2xm\angle AOC=2xm∠AOC=2x and m∠BOC=3xm\angle BOC=3xm∠BOC=3x. Find xxx and each angle.
Solution
∠AOC\angle AOC∠AOC and ∠BOC\angle BOC∠BOC form a straight (linear) angle along the diameter, so they sum to 180∘180^\circ180∘:
2x+3x=180∘2x+3x=180^\circ2x+3x=180∘
5x=180∘⇒x=36∘5x=180^\circ \Rightarrow x=36^\circ5x=180∘⇒x=36∘.
m∠AOC=2(36)=72∘m\angle AOC=2(36)=72^\circm∠AOC=2(36)=72∘; m∠BOC=3(36)=108∘m\angle BOC=3(36)=108^\circm∠BOC=3(36)=108∘.
Answer: x=36∘x=36^\circx=36∘; angles 72∘72^\circ72∘ and 108∘108^\circ108∘.
Example 5: Testing the longest-chord rule
A circle has radius 999. Is a chord of length 202020 possible?
Solution
No chord can be longer than the diameter — the diameter is the longest chord.
The diameter is 2(9)=182(9)=182(9)=18, and no chord can exceed the diameter.
Since 20>1820>1820>18, no such chord is possible.
Example 6: Radius from diameter
A circle has diameter 141414. Find the radius.
Solution
Identify the given value: d=14d=14d=14.
The radius is always half the diameter:
r=d2=142=7r=\dfrac{d}{2}=\dfrac{14}{2}=7r=2d​=214​=7.
Answer: 777.
Example 7: Central angle from an arc
Minor arc PQPQPQ measures 130∘130^\circ130∘. Find the central angle that intercepts it.
Solution
A central angle equals its intercepted arc: m∠POQ=130∘m\angle POQ=130^\circm∠POQ=130∘.
Answer: 130∘130^\circ130∘.
Example 8: Semicircle
Chord AB‾\overline{AB}AB is a diameter of the circle. What is mAB^m\widehat{AB}mAB (the arc on either side)?
Solution
A diameter divides the circle into two semicircles, each measuring 180∘180^\circ180∘: mAB^=180∘m\widehat{AB}=180^\circmAB=180∘.
Answer: 180∘180^\circ180∘.
Example 9: Chord vs. diameter
A circle has radius 6.56.56.5. What is the longest possible chord?
Solution
The diameter is the longest chord: d=2(6.5)=13d=2(6.5)=13d=2(6.5)=13.
Answer: 131313.
Example 10: Central angles in a given ratio
Four central angles around a point are in the ratio 1:2:3:41:2:3:41:2:3:4. Find each angle.
Solution
Let the angles be xxx, 2x2x2x, 3x3x3x, 4x4x4x. They sum to 360∘360^\circ360∘:
x+2x+3x+4x=360∘x+2x+3x+4x=360^\circx+2x+3x+4x=360∘
10x=360∘⇒x=36∘10x=360^\circ \Rightarrow x=36^\circ10x=360∘⇒x=36∘.
Angles: 36∘36^\circ36∘, 72∘72^\circ72∘, 108∘108^\circ108∘, 144∘144^\circ144∘.
Check: 36+72+108+144=360∘36+72+108+144=360^\circ36+72+108+144=360∘. ✓
Example 11: Application — a clock face
A clock face is a circle with the center at the pivot of its hands. Find the central angle (and the corresponding arc) from the 121212 to the 444.
Solution
A clock's 121212 hour marks divide the circle into 121212 equal central angles: each is 360∘12=30∘\dfrac{360^\circ}{12}=30^\circ12360∘​=30∘.
From 121212 to 444 spans 444 of these equal angles: 4(30∘)=120∘4(30^\circ)=120^\circ4(30∘)=120∘.
Since the central angle equals its intercepted arc, the arc from 121212 to 444 also measures 120∘120^\circ120∘.
Answer: 120∘120^\circ120∘.
