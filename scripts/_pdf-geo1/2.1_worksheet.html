2.1 Lines and Angles
A transversal crossing two lines creates eight angles, and the names — corresponding, alternate interior, alternate exterior, same-side interior — describe exact positional relationships, not vague location. Learning to read these positions correctly (interior vs. exterior, same side vs. opposite side of the transversal) is what makes Section 2.2's parallel-line theorems almost automatic once you get there. This section pushes past simple recall by asking you to classify a pair from a description, count angles across multiple lines, and work out every relationship a single angle has with all seven others.

Examples

Example 1: Setting up the figure
Line ttt is a transversal crossing lines ℓ\ellℓ and mmm, creating angles 111–888 as shown. List which angles are interior and which are exterior.
Solution
Transversal t crosses lines ℓ and m, forming 8 angles.
A transversal is a line that crosses two or more coplanar lines at distinct points — here that's ttt, crossing ℓ\ellℓ and mmm.
Interior angles lie between ℓ\ellℓ and mmm: at the ℓ\ellℓ crossing those are ∠3,∠4\angle3,\angle4∠3,∠4; at the mmm crossing, ∠5,∠6\angle5,\angle6∠5,∠6.
Exterior angles lie outside the two lines: ∠1,∠2\angle1,\angle2∠1,∠2 (above ℓ\ellℓ) and ∠7,∠8\angle7,\angle8∠7,∠8 (below mmm).
Answer: Interior: ∠3,∠4,∠5,∠6\angle3,\angle4,\angle5,\angle6∠3,∠4,∠5,∠6; Exterior: ∠1,∠2,∠7,∠8\angle1,\angle2,\angle7,\angle8∠1,∠2,∠7,∠8.
Example 2: Corresponding angles
Explain what makes two angles "corresponding," and name all four corresponding pairs in the figure.
Solution
Corresponding angles occupy the same relative position at each intersection — same side of the transversal, and the same rotational corner, just at a different crossing.
Matching corners: top-left↔top-left, top-right↔top-right, bottom-left↔bottom-left, bottom-right↔bottom-right.
Answer: (∠1,∠5)(\angle1,\angle5)(∠1,∠5), (∠2,∠6)(\angle2,\angle6)(∠2,∠6), (∠3,∠7)(\angle3,\angle7)(∠3,∠7), (∠4,∠8)(\angle4,\angle8)(∠4,∠8).
Example 3: Alternate interior angles
Which two pairs of angles are alternate interior angles? State the two conditions they must satisfy.
Solution
∠3, ∠4 are interior at ℓ; ∠5, ∠6 are interior at m.
Two conditions must both hold: (1) both angles are interior (between ℓ\ellℓ and mmm), and (2) they lie on opposite sides of the transversal.
Interior angles are ∠3,∠4\angle3,\angle4∠3,∠4 (at ℓ\ellℓ) and ∠5,∠6\angle5,\angle6∠5,∠6 (at mmm); pairing opposite sides gives ∠3\angle3∠3 (left) with ∠6\angle6∠6 (right), and ∠4\angle4∠4 (right) with ∠5\angle5∠5 (left).
Answer: (∠3,∠6)(\angle3,\angle6)(∠3,∠6) and (∠4,∠5)(\angle4,\angle5)(∠4,∠5).
Example 4: Same-side interior angles
Which two pairs of angles are same-side (consecutive) interior angles?
Solution
Same two "interior" angles as before, but now paired on the same side of the transversal instead of opposite sides.
∠3\angle3∠3 (left, at ℓ\ellℓ) pairs with ∠5\angle5∠5 (left, at mmm); ∠4\angle4∠4 (right, at ℓ\ellℓ) pairs with ∠6\angle6∠6 (right, at mmm).
Answer: (∠3,∠5)(\angle3,\angle5)(∠3,∠5) and (∠4,∠6)(\angle4,\angle6)(∠4,∠6).
Example 5: Alternate exterior angles
Which two pairs of angles are alternate exterior angles?
Solution
Exterior angles are ∠1,∠2\angle1,\angle2∠1,∠2 (above ℓ\ellℓ) and ∠7,∠8\angle7,\angle8∠7,∠8 (below mmm); alternate means opposite sides of the transversal.
∠1\angle1∠1 (left, exterior) pairs with ∠8\angle8∠8 (right, exterior); ∠2\angle2∠2 (right, exterior) pairs with ∠7\angle7∠7 (left, exterior).
Answer: (∠1,∠8)(\angle1,\angle8)(∠1,∠8) and (∠2,∠7)(\angle2,\angle7)(∠2,∠7).
Example 6: Vertical and linear pairs at one intersection
At the intersection of ttt and ℓ\ellℓ alone, name the vertical pair(s) and all linear pairs among ∠1,∠2,∠3,∠4\angle1,\angle2,\angle3,\angle4∠1,∠2,∠3,∠4.
Solution
Two lines crossing always make 2 vertical pairs and 4 linear pairs.
At any single intersection of two lines, four angles are formed, giving 2 pairs directly across from each other (vertical) and 4 adjacent pairs (linear pairs).
Vertical: ∠1&∠4\angle1 \& \angle4∠1&∠4, and ∠2&∠3\angle2 \& \angle3∠2&∠3.
Linear pairs (adjacent, sum to 180∘180^\circ180∘): (∠1,∠2)(\angle1,\angle2)(∠1,∠2), (∠2,∠4)(\angle2,\angle4)(∠2,∠4), (∠4,∠3)(\angle4,\angle3)(∠4,∠3), (∠3,∠1)(\angle3,\angle1)(∠3,∠1).
Note: this is true at any intersection of two lines — the transversal isn't even needed for this fact.
Example 7: Counting angles with more lines
A transversal crosses 333 distinct parallel lines (not just 222). How many angles are formed in total, and how many at each intersection?
Solution
Each place the transversal meets one of the lines is a single intersection of two lines, and two crossing lines always create 444 angles.
With 333 lines being crossed, there are 333 separate intersections.
Total angles =4×3=12= 4 \times 3 = 12=4×3=12.
Answer: 121212 angles total — 444 at each of the 333 intersections.
Example 8: Interior vs. exterior by coordinates
Horizontal lines ℓ:y=6\ell: y=6ℓ:y=6 and m:y=2m: y=2m:y=2 are cut by a transversal. A point on the transversal has yyy-coordinate 444. Is the angle at that point interior or exterior? What about a point with y=8y = 8y=8?
Solution
Interior means between the two lines: since 2<4<62 < 4 < 62<4<6, the point at y=4y=4y=4 sits between mmm and ℓ\ellℓ — interior.
Since 8>68 > 68>6, the point at y=8y=8y=8 is above both lines, outside the strip between them — exterior.
Answer: y=4y=4y=4: interior; y=8y=8y=8: exterior.
Example 9: Classify from a description
∠4\angle4∠4 and ∠5\angle5∠5 are both interior angles, and they lie on the same side of transversal ttt. What are they called, and what will Section 2.2 say about them if ℓ∥m\ell \parallel mℓ∥m?
Solution
Both interior + same side of the transversal matches exactly one name: same-side interior angles (also called co-interior or consecutive interior angles).
Preview: once ℓ∥m\ell \parallel mℓ∥m is established, Section 2.2 proves that same-side interior angles are supplementary — unlike corresponding or alternate pairs, which turn out to be congruent instead.
Answer: Same-side interior angles.
Example 10: All seven relationships of one angle
For ∠2\angle2∠2 in the figure, classify its relationship to each of ∠1,∠3,∠4,∠5,∠6,∠7,∠8\angle1, \angle3, \angle4, \angle5, \angle6, \angle7, \angle8∠1,∠3,∠4,∠5,∠6,∠7,∠8.
Solution
Work outward from ∠2 to every other numbered angle.
Go through each angle relative to ∠2\angle2∠2 (top-right, exterior): ∠1\angle1∠1 is adjacent at ℓ\ellℓ with outer sides forming a line — linear pair. ∠3\angle3∠3 is directly across at ℓ\ellℓ — vertical. ∠4\angle4∠4 is the other angle adjacent to ∠2\angle2∠2 at ℓ\ellℓ — linear pair.
∠5\angle5∠5 is on the opposite side of ttt from ∠2\angle2∠2 and a different interior/exterior status — no standard named relationship. ∠6\angle6∠6 is the same corner position at the other intersection — corresponding. ∠7\angle7∠7 is exterior, opposite side of ttt — alternate exterior. ∠8\angle8∠8 is exterior, same side of ttt — same-side exterior.
Answer: linear pair: ∠1,∠4\angle1,\angle4∠1,∠4; vertical: ∠3\angle3∠3; corresponding: ∠6\angle6∠6; alternate exterior: ∠7\angle7∠7; same-side exterior: ∠8\angle8∠8; no special relationship: ∠5\angle5∠5.
