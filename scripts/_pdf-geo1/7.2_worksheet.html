7.2 Properties of Parallelograms
A parallelogram is a quadrilateral with both pairs of opposite sides parallel. From that one fact, four powerful properties follow: opposite sides are congruent, opposite angles are congruent, consecutive angles are supplementary (180∘180^\circ180∘ total), and the diagonals bisect each other. These properties come from parallel lines cut by a transversal (each side of a parallelogram acts as a transversal across the opposite pair of parallel sides) and from congruent triangles formed by a diagonal. This worksheet uses all four properties together — often two variables in one problem — and asks you to justify, not just compute.

Examples

Example 1: Why consecutive angles are supplementary
In parallelogram ABCDABCDABCD, AB‾∥DC‾\overline{AB}\parallel\overline{DC}AB∥DC, and AD‾\overline{AD}AD is a transversal crossing both. Explain why m∠A+m∠D=180∘m\angle A + m\angle D = 180^\circm∠A+m∠D=180∘.
Solution
AB‾∥DC‾\overline{AB}\parallel\overline{DC}AB∥DC; side AD‾\overline{AD}AD acts as a transversal between them.
Since AB‾∥DC‾\overline{AB}\parallel\overline{DC}AB∥DC, the transversal AD‾\overline{AD}AD creates a pair of co-interior (same-side interior) angles at vertices AAA and DDD.
Co-interior angles formed by a transversal across parallel lines are always supplementary, so m∠A+m∠D=180∘m\angle A+m\angle D=180^\circm∠A+m∠D=180∘.
The same reasoning applies to every pair of consecutive vertices in a parallelogram, since each side is parallel to the side across from it. This is why consecutive angles in any parallelogram are supplementary.
Example 2: Find all four angles
In parallelogram ABCDABCDABCD, m∠A=110∘m\angle A = 110^\circm∠A=110∘. Find m∠Bm\angle Bm∠B, m∠Cm\angle Cm∠C, and m∠Dm\angle Dm∠D.
Solution
Opposite angles A,CA,CA,C are congruent; consecutive angles like A,BA,BA,B are supplementary.
Opposite angles are congruent: m∠C=m∠A=110∘m\angle C = m\angle A = 110^\circm∠C=m∠A=110∘.
Consecutive angles are supplementary: m∠B=180∘−110∘=70∘m\angle B = 180^\circ - 110^\circ = 70^\circm∠B=180∘−110∘=70∘.
m∠D=m∠B=70∘m\angle D = m\angle B = 70^\circm∠D=m∠B=70∘ (opposite angles).
Check: 110+70+110+70=360∘110+70+110+70=360^\circ110+70+110+70=360∘, the quadrilateral angle sum. ✓
Answer: ∠B=70∘\angle B=70^\circ∠B=70∘, ∠C=110∘\angle C=110^\circ∠C=110∘, ∠D=70∘\angle D=70^\circ∠D=70∘.
Example 3: Diagonals bisecting each other
Diagonals of parallelogram ABCDABCDABCD intersect at OOO. If AC=20AC=20AC=20 and BO=8BO=8BO=8, find AOAOAO and the full length of BDBDBD.
Solution
Point OOO is the midpoint of both diagonals.
Diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other, so OOO is the midpoint of AC‾\overline{AC}AC: AO=AC2=202=10AO=\dfrac{AC}{2}=\dfrac{20}{2}=10AO=2AC​=220​=10.
OOO is also the midpoint of BD‾\overline{BD}BD, so BD=2⋅BO=2(8)=16BD=2\cdot BO=2(8)=16BD=2⋅BO=2(8)=16.
Answer: AO=10AO=10AO=10; BD=16BD=16BD=16.

Example 5: Solve for $x$ using diagonal segments

Example 6: Two-step opposite-angle problem
In parallelogram ABCDABCDABCD, ∠A=4x\angle A=4x∠A=4x and ∠C=3x+20\angle C=3x+20∠C=3x+20. Find xxx, then find ∠A\angle A∠A and the consecutive angle ∠B\angle B∠B.
Solution
Opposite angles are congruent: 4x=3x+20⇒x=204x=3x+20 \Rightarrow x=204x=3x+20⇒x=20.
∠A=4(20)=80∘\angle A=4(20)=80^\circ∠A=4(20)=80∘.

Answer: x=20x=20x=20; ∠A=80∘\angle A=80^\circ∠A=80∘; ∠B=100∘\angle B=100^\circ∠B=100∘.
Example 7: Solve for a side, then find the perimeter
In parallelogram ABCDABCDABCD, AB=5y−2AB=5y-2AB=5y−2 and CD=3y+8CD=3y+8CD=3y+8. If AD=15AD=15AD=15, find yyy, ABABAB, and the perimeter of ABCDABCDABCD.

AB=5(5)−2=23AB=5(5)-2=23AB=5(5)−2=23, and BC=AD=15BC=AD=15BC=AD=15 (opposite sides).
Perimeter =2(AB+AD)=2(23+15)=2(38)=76=2(AB+AD)=2(23+15)=2(38)=76=2(AB+AD)=2(23+15)=2(38)=76.
Answer: y=5y=5y=5; AB=23AB=23AB=23; perimeter =76=76=76.
Example 8: Can these be consecutive angles?
A student claims a parallelogram can have consecutive angles of 95∘95^\circ95∘ and 90∘90^\circ90∘. Is this possible?
Solution
Consecutive angles of a parallelogram must be supplementary, summing to exactly 180∘180^\circ180∘.
95∘+90∘=185∘≠180∘95^\circ+90^\circ=185^\circ \neq 180^\circ95∘+90∘=185∘=180∘.
Answer: No — the angles don't satisfy the supplementary requirement, so no such parallelogram exists.

Example 10: Application — crate lid brace
A rectangular-shipping-crate lid is actually a slightly skewed parallelogram panel ABCDABCDABCD with AB=42AB=42AB=42 cm. A diagonal support brace along AC‾\overline{AC}AC measures 909090 cm and crosses the other diagonal at OOO, where BO=37BO=37BO=37 cm. Find CDCDCD and the full length of diagonal BDBDBD.
Solution
Opposite sides are congruent, so CD=AB=42CD=AB=42CD=AB=42 cm.
Diagonals bisect each other, so OOO is the midpoint of BD‾\overline{BD}BD: BD=2⋅BO=2(37)=74BD=2\cdot BO=2(37)=74BD=2⋅BO=2(37)=74 cm.
(Note: AC=90AC=90AC=90 cm was extra information confirming the diagonal length, not needed to find CDCDCD or BDBDBD.)
Answer: CD=42CD=42CD=42 cm; BD=74BD=74BD=74 cm.
