1.3 Measuring and Constructing Angles
Angle measure turns a picture of two rays into a number you can compute with, the same way segment length did for two points. The Angle Addition Postulate and the definition of an angle bisector let you build equations with a variable buried inside a measure, not just read a protractor. This section also connects back to earlier ideas — opposite rays make a straight angle, and a bisector's two halves are congruent — so several problems ask you to justify a step, not only find a number.

Examples

Example 1: Classify by range
Classify ∠A\angle A∠A if m∠A=47∘m\angle A = 47^\circm∠A=47∘.
Solution
An angle less than 90° is acute.
Recall the ranges: acute is 0∘0^\circ0∘ to 90∘90^\circ90∘, right is exactly 90∘90^\circ90∘, obtuse is 90∘90^\circ90∘ to 180∘180^\circ180∘, straight is exactly 180∘180^\circ180∘.
Compare: 47∘47^\circ47∘ falls between 0∘0^\circ0∘ and 90∘90^\circ90∘.
Answer: ∠A\angle A∠A is acute.
Example 2: Right angle
What is the measure of a right angle, and how is it marked in a diagram?
Solution
By definition, a right angle is formed by two perpendicular rays.
A right angle is always drawn with a small square at the vertex, not a curved arc — that square is the symbol for 90∘90^\circ90∘.
Answer: 90∘90^\circ90∘.
Example 3: Straight angle
What is the measure of a straight angle, and how do its two sides relate to Section 1.1's opposite rays?
Solution
A straight angle's two sides are opposite rays — they share the vertex and point in exactly opposite directions, forming one straight line.
By definition, a straight angle measures exactly 180∘180^\circ180∘.
Answer: 180∘180^\circ180∘.
Example 4: Angle Addition Postulate
DDD is in the interior of ∠ABC\angle ABC∠ABC. If m∠ABD=35∘m\angle ABD = 35^\circm∠ABD=35∘ and m∠DBC=50∘m\angle DBC = 50^\circm∠DBC=50∘, find m∠ABCm\angle ABCm∠ABC.
Solution
D is inside angle ABC, splitting it into two smaller angles.
Identify the relationship: since DDD is in the interior of ∠ABC\angle ABC∠ABC, the Angle Addition Postulate applies: m∠ABD+m∠DBC=m∠ABCm\angle ABD + m\angle DBC = m\angle ABCm∠ABD+m∠DBC=m∠ABC.
Substitute: 35∘+50∘=m∠ABC35^\circ + 50^\circ = m\angle ABC35∘+50∘=m∠ABC.
Answer: m∠ABC=85∘m\angle ABC = 85^\circm∠ABC=85∘.
Example 5: Find a missing part
m∠ABC=110∘m\angle ABC = 110^\circm∠ABC=110∘ and m∠ABD=70∘m\angle ABD = 70^\circm∠ABD=70∘, with DDD interior to ∠ABC\angle ABC∠ABC. Find m∠DBCm\angle DBCm∠DBC.
Solution
Start from the same postulate, solving for the smaller piece instead of the whole: m∠ABD+m∠DBC=m∠ABCm\angle ABD + m\angle DBC = m\angle ABCm∠ABD+m∠DBC=m∠ABC.
70∘+m∠DBC=110∘70^\circ + m\angle DBC = 110^\circ70∘+m∠DBC=110∘
Answer: m∠DBC=40∘m\angle DBC = 40^\circm∠DBC=40∘.
Example 6: Angle bisector
BD→\overrightarrow{BD}BD bisects ∠ABC\angle ABC∠ABC. If m∠ABD=28∘m\angle ABD = 28^\circm∠ABD=28∘, find m∠ABCm\angle ABCm∠ABC.
Solution
A bisector splits the angle into two congruent halves.
Definition: a bisector splits an angle into two congruent halves, so m∠ABD=m∠DBC=28∘m\angle ABD = m\angle DBC = 28^\circm∠ABD=m∠DBC=28∘.
m∠ABC=m∠ABD+m∠DBC=28∘+28∘m\angle ABC = m\angle ABD + m\angle DBC = 28^\circ + 28^\circm∠ABC=m∠ABD+m∠DBC=28∘+28∘.
(Shortcut for any bisector problem: m∠ABC=2⋅m∠ABDm\angle ABC = 2\cdot m\angle ABDm∠ABC=2⋅m∠ABD.)
Answer: 56∘56^\circ56∘.
Example 7: Bisector algebra
BD→\overrightarrow{BD}BD bisects ∠ABC\angle ABC∠ABC. If m∠ABD=3x+2m\angle ABD = 3x+2m∠ABD=3x+2 and m∠DBC=5x−10m\angle DBC = 5x-10m∠DBC=5x−10, find xxx.
Solution
A bisector means the two halves are equal: m∠ABD=m∠DBCm\angle ABD = m\angle DBCm∠ABD=m∠DBC.
3x+2=5x−103x+2 = 5x-103x+2=5x−10
12=2x⇒x=612 = 2x \Rightarrow x = 612=2x⇒x=6.
Check: both halves equal 3(6)+2=20∘3(6)+2 = 20^\circ3(6)+2=20∘. ✓
Example 8: Classify and preview supplements
Classify ∠Q\angle Q∠Q if m∠Q=125∘m\angle Q = 125^\circm∠Q=125∘, and find its supplement.
Solution
Compare to the ranges: 90∘<125∘<180∘90^\circ < 125^\circ < 180^\circ90∘<125∘<180∘, so ∠Q\angle Q∠Q is obtuse.
Its supplement is 180∘−125∘=55∘180^\circ - 125^\circ = 55^\circ180∘−125∘=55∘, which is acute — a preview of Section 1.4, where supplementary pairs are studied directly.
Answer: obtuse; supplement 55∘55^\circ55∘.
Example 9: Solve with Angle Addition
DDD is interior to ∠ABC\angle ABC∠ABC. m∠ABD=2xm\angle ABD = 2xm∠ABD=2x, m∠DBC=x+15m\angle DBC = x + 15m∠DBC=x+15, and m∠ABC=75∘m\angle ABC = 75^\circm∠ABC=75∘. Find xxx.
Solution
The two algebraic angle pieces must add to the given whole angle.
Write the postulate as an equation: m∠ABD+m∠DBC=m∠ABCm\angle ABD + m\angle DBC = m\angle ABCm∠ABD+m∠DBC=m∠ABC.
2x+(x+15)=752x + (x+15) = 752x+(x+15)=75
3x+15=75⇒3x=60⇒x=203x+15 = 75 \Rightarrow 3x = 60 \Rightarrow x = 203x+15=75⇒3x=60⇒x=20.
Check: m∠ABD=40∘m\angle ABD = 40^\circm∠ABD=40∘, m∠DBC=35∘m\angle DBC = 35^\circm∠DBC=35∘, and 40∘+35∘=75∘40^\circ+35^\circ=75^\circ40∘+35∘=75∘. ✓
Example 10: Reading a protractor
A protractor's center is placed at vertex BBB. Ray BABABA lines up with the 20∘20^\circ20∘ mark and ray BCBCBC lines up with the 145∘145^\circ145∘ mark on the same scale. Find m∠ABCm\angle ABCm∠ABC and classify it.
Solution
On a single protractor scale, the measure of the angle between two rays is the positive difference of their readings.
m∠ABC=∣145∘−20∘∣=125∘m\angle ABC = |145^\circ - 20^\circ| = 125^\circm∠ABC=∣145∘−20∘∣=125∘.
Since 90∘<125∘<180∘90^\circ < 125^\circ < 180^\circ90∘<125∘<180∘, the angle is obtuse.
Answer: 125∘125^\circ125∘, obtuse.
