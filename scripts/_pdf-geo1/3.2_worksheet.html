3.2 Reflections
A reflection flips a figure over a mirror line so that every point and its image are the same distance from the line, on opposite sides — which is why reflections preserve distance but reverse orientation, unlike translations. Beyond the four memorized rules (xxx-axis, yyy-axis, y=xy=xy=x, y=−xy=-xy=−x), this section covers reflecting over any horizontal or vertical line using a distance formula, proving algebraically why distance survives while orientation flips, and working backward to find the mirror line itself from a point and its image.

Examples

Example 1: Reflect over the $x$-axis
Reflect (3,5)(3, 5)(3,5) over the xxx-axis.
Solution
Flipping over the x-axis keeps x the same and negates y.
Reflecting over the xxx-axis keeps xxx the same and negates yyy — the point flips vertically, landing the same distance below the axis as it started above.
(3,5)→(3,−5)(3,5) \rightarrow (3,-5)(3,5)→(3,−5).
Answer: (3,−5)(3, -5)(3,−5).
Example 2: Reflect over the $y$-axis
Reflect (3,5)(3, 5)(3,5) over the yyy-axis.
Solution
Reflecting over the yyy-axis keeps yyy the same and negates xxx.
(3,5)→(−3,5)(3,5) \rightarrow (-3,5)(3,5)→(−3,5).
Answer: (−3,5)(-3, 5)(−3,5).
Example 3: Reflect over $y = x$
Reflect (2,7)(2, 7)(2,7) over y=xy = xy=x.
Solution
Reflecting over y = x swaps the coordinates.
The mirror line y=xy=xy=x treats horizontal and vertical distance symmetrically, so reflecting over it swaps the coordinates.
(2,7)→(7,2)(2,7) \rightarrow (7,2)(2,7)→(7,2).
Answer: (7,2)(7, 2)(7,2).
Example 4: Reflect over $y = -x$
Reflect (4,1)(4, 1)(4,1) over y=−xy = -xy=−x.
Solution
Reflecting over y=−xy=-xy=−x swaps the coordinates and negates both (the extra negation accounts for the line's negative slope).
(4,1)→(−1,−4)(4,1) \rightarrow (-1,-4)(4,1)→(−1,−4).
Answer: (−1,−4)(-1, -4)(−1,−4).
Example 5: Reflect over a vertical line
Reflect (1,3)(1, 3)(1,3) over the line x=2x = 2x=2.
Solution
For a vertical mirror at x=kx=kx=k, yyy stays the same, and the new xxx-coordinate is found with x′=2k−xx' = 2k - xx′=2k−x (the image is as far past kkk as the original was before it).
x′=2(2)−1=3x' = 2(2) - 1 = 3x′=2(2)−1=3.
Answer: (3,3)(3, 3)(3,3).
Example 6: Reflect over a horizontal line
Reflect (5,1)(5, 1)(5,1) over the line y=3y = 3y=3.
Solution
For a horizontal mirror at y=ky=ky=k, xxx stays the same, and y′=2k−yy' = 2k - yy′=2k−y.
y′=2(3)−1=5y' = 2(3) - 1 = 5y′=2(3)−1=5.
Answer: (5,5)(5, 5)(5,5).
Example 7: Distance preserved, orientation reversed
Explain why a reflection preserves distances between points but reverses orientation.
Solution
Reflecting over the xxx-axis sends (x,y)→(x,−y)(x,y)\to(x,-y)(x,y)→(x,−y). For two points P1=(x1,y1)P_1=(x_1,y_1)P1​=(x1​,y1​), P2=(x2,y2)P_2=(x_2,y_2)P2​=(x2​,y2​), the horizontal difference x2−x1x_2-x_1x2​−x1​ is unchanged, and the vertical difference becomes −(y2−y1)-(y_2-y_1)−(y2​−y1​) — same size, opposite sign — so the (squared) distance formula gives an identical result.
Orientation flips because a mirror image is like viewing a shape through glass: tracing clockwise around the original becomes counterclockwise around the image whenever exactly one coordinate direction is negated.
Answer: Distance survives because squaring erases the sign flip; orientation reverses because exactly one direction is negated.
Example 8: Reflect a whole triangle
△ABC\triangle ABC△ABC has A(1,1)A(1,1)A(1,1), B(4,1)B(4,1)B(4,1), C(1,3)C(1,3)C(1,3). Reflect over the yyy-axis. Find all three image vertices.
Solution
Every vertex negates its x-coordinate.
Negate the xxx-coordinate of each vertex, keeping yyy the same: A′=(−1,1)A'=(-1,1)A′=(−1,1); B′=(−4,1)B'=(-4,1)B′=(−4,1); C′=(−1,3)C'=(-1,3)C′=(−1,3).
Answer: A′(−1,1)A'(-1,1)A′(−1,1), B′(−4,1)B'(-4,1)B′(−4,1), C′(−1,3)C'(-1,3)C′(−1,3) — the mirror image, same size and shape.
Example 9: Find the line of reflection
A point (2,3)(2,3)(2,3) reflects to (2,9)(2,9)(2,9). Find the equation of the line of reflection.
Solution
The mirror line is the perpendicular bisector of the segment joining a point and its image.
The line of reflection is always the perpendicular bisector of the segment joining a point and its image.
Both points share x=2x=2x=2, so the segment is vertical, making its perpendicular bisector horizontal.
Find the midpoint's yyy-coordinate: y=3+92=6y = \dfrac{3+9}{2} = 6y=23+9​=6.
Answer: The line of reflection is y=6y = 6y=6.
Example 10: Reflecting twice over the same line
A point is reflected over the xxx-axis, then that image is reflected over the xxx-axis again. What is the net result, and why?
Solution
First reflection: (x,y)→(x,−y)(x,y) \rightarrow (x,-y)(x,y)→(x,−y). Second reflection over the same line: (x,−y)→(x,−(−y))=(x,y)(x,-y) \rightarrow (x, -(-y)) = (x,y)(x,−y)→(x,−(−y))=(x,y).
The two sign flips cancel, returning every point to its original location.
Answer: The net result is the identity transformation — reflecting twice over the same line always undoes itself, a preview of composed transformations in Section 3.4.
