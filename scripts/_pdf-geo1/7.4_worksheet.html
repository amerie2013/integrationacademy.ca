7.4 Trapezoids and Kites
A trapezoid has exactly one pair of parallel sides, called the bases. Its midsegment (median) connects the midpoints of the two legs, runs parallel to both bases, and has length equal to the average of the two base lengths: m=b1+b22m=\dfrac{b_1+b_2}{2}m=2b1​+b2​​. An isosceles trapezoid (congruent legs) has an extra pair of properties: both base angles at each base are congruent to each other, and the diagonals are congruent. A kite has two distinct pairs of consecutive congruent sides (not opposite, like a parallelogram). Its diagonals are always perpendicular; the diagonal connecting the vertices between the congruent sides is the axis of symmetry — it bisects the other diagonal and bisects the two vertex angles it passes through, while the two angles between the unequal sides are congruent to each other. This worksheet uses all of these relationships together.

Examples

Example 1: Midsegment from two bases
A trapezoid has bases 101010 and 161616. Find the length of the midsegment.
Solution
The midsegment joins the midpoints of the legs and is parallel to both bases.
Identify the two bases: b1=10b_1=10b1​=10, b2=16b_2=16b2​=16.
The midsegment is the average of the two bases:
m=b1+b22=10+162=262=13m=\dfrac{b_1+b_2}{2}=\dfrac{10+16}{2}=\dfrac{26}{2}=13m=2b1​+b2​​=210+16​=226​=13.
Answer: 131313.
Example 2: Find a missing base
A trapezoid has midsegment 141414 and one base 999. Find the other base.
Solution
Identify the known midsegment and base: m=14m=14m=14, b1=9b_1=9b1​=9, and set up the midsegment equation with b2=bb_2=bb2​=b unknown.
Solve for the missing base:
9+b2=14\dfrac{9+b}{2}=1429+b​=14
9+b=289+b=289+b=28
b=19b=19b=19.
Answer: 191919.
Example 3: Isosceles trapezoid base angles
An isosceles trapezoid has base angles of 70∘70^\circ70∘ at its longer base. Find the two angles at the shorter base.
Solution
In an isosceles trapezoid, both base angles at the same base are congruent, and each pair of angles sharing a leg is supplementary (co-interior angles between the parallel bases).
Angle at the shorter base =180∘−70∘=110∘=180^\circ-70^\circ=110^\circ=180∘−70∘=110∘.
By symmetry, both angles at the shorter base equal 110∘110^\circ110∘.
Answer: 110∘110^\circ110∘ and 110∘110^\circ110∘.
Example 4: Isosceles trapezoid diagonals are congruent
In isosceles trapezoid ABCDABCDABCD, diagonal AC=5x−2AC=5x-2AC=5x−2 and diagonal BD=3x+8BD=3x+8BD=3x+8. Find xxx and the length of each diagonal.
Solution
Diagonals of an isosceles trapezoid are congruent: AC=BDAC=BDAC=BD.
5x−2=3x+85x-2=3x+85x−2=3x+8
2x=10⇒x=52x=10 \Rightarrow x=52x=10⇒x=5.
AC=5(5)−2=23AC=5(5)-2=23AC=5(5)−2=23.
Answer: x=5x=5x=5; each diagonal =23=23=23.
Example 5: Kite angle sum
In kite ABCDABCDABCD (with AB=ADAB=ADAB=AD and CB=CDCB=CDCB=CD), the two congruent non-vertex angles are m∠B=m∠D=100∘m\angle B=m\angle D=100^\circm∠B=m∠D=100∘, and m∠A=40∘m\angle A=40^\circm∠A=40∘. Find m∠Cm\angle Cm∠C.
Solution
AB=ADAB=ADAB=AD and CB=CDCB=CDCB=CD; the axis AC‾\overline{AC}AC splits the kite into two congruent triangles.
All four angles of any quadrilateral sum to 360∘360^\circ360∘:
40+100+100+m∠C=36040+100+100+m\angle C=36040+100+100+m∠C=360

Example 6: Kite area from diagonals
A kite has diagonals 121212 and 161616. Find its area.
Solution
A kite's diagonals are perpendicular, so its area is half the product of the diagonals — just like a rhombus:
A=12(12)(16)=12(192)=96A=\dfrac{1}{2}(12)(16)=\dfrac{1}{2}(192)=96A=21​(12)(16)=21​(192)=96.
Answer: 969696.
Example 7: Isosceles trapezoid legs
An isosceles trapezoid has legs 3x+23x+23x+2 and x+10x+10x+10. Find xxx and the length of each leg.
Solution
The legs of an isosceles trapezoid are congruent:
3x+2=x+103x+2=x+103x+2=x+10
2x=8⇒x=42x=8 \Rightarrow x=42x=8⇒x=4.
Each leg =3(4)+2=14=3(4)+2=14=3(4)+2=14.
Answer: x=4x=4x=4; each leg =14=14=14.
Example 8: Kite perimeter
Kite ABCDABCDABCD has AB=AD=8AB=AD=8AB=AD=8 and CB=CD=5CB=CD=5CB=CD=5. Find the perimeter.
Solution
Identify the two pairs of consecutive congruent sides: AB=AD=8AB=AD=8AB=AD=8 and CB=CD=5CB=CD=5CB=CD=5.
Add all four sides:
Perimeter =AB+BC+CD+DA=8+5+5+8=26=AB+BC+CD+DA=8+5+5+8=26=AB+BC+CD+DA=8+5+5+8=26.
Answer: 262626.
Example 9: Trapezoid consecutive angles along a leg
In trapezoid ABCDABCDABCD, AB‾∥DC‾\overline{AB}\parallel\overline{DC}AB∥DC, and m∠A=95∘m\angle A=95^\circm∠A=95∘. Find m∠Dm\angle Dm∠D (the angle sharing leg AD‾\overline{AD}AD with ∠A\angle A∠A).
Solution
∠A\angle A∠A and ∠D\angle D∠D are co-interior (same-side interior) angles formed by transversal AD‾\overline{AD}AD across the parallel bases, so they are supplementary:
m∠D=180∘−95∘=85∘m\angle D=180^\circ-95^\circ=85^\circm∠D=180∘−95∘=85∘.
Answer: 85∘85^\circ85∘.
Example 10: Application — window brace
A trapezoidal window frame has a horizontal brace connecting the midpoints of its two slanted sides (legs). The bottom base is 343434 in, and the brace measures 242424 in. Find the length of the top base.
Solution
The brace is the midsegment of the trapezoid, so it equals the average of the two bases:
34+t2=24\dfrac{34+t}{2}=24234+t​=24
34+t=4834+t=4834+t=48
t=14t=14t=14.
Answer: the top base is 141414 in.
Example 11: Kite axis bisects the vertex angle
In kite ABCDABCDABCD with AB=ADAB=ADAB=AD and CB=CDCB=CDCB=CD, diagonal AC‾\overline{AC}AC is the axis of symmetry. If m∠A=68∘m\angle A=68^\circm∠A=68∘, find m∠BACm\angle BACm∠BAC.
Solution
The axis of symmetry AC‾\overline{AC}AC bisects the vertex angles it passes through (here, ∠A\angle A∠A and ∠C\angle C∠C), splitting each into two congruent parts:
m∠BAC=68∘2=34∘m\angle BAC=\dfrac{68^\circ}{2}=34^\circm∠BAC=268∘​=34∘.
Answer: 34∘34^\circ34∘.
