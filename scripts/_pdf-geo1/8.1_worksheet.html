8.1 Distance, Midpoint, and Slope
Three formulas describe a segment completely on the coordinate plane. The distance formula, d=(x2−x1)2+(y2−y1)2d=\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2}d=(x2​−x1​)2+(y2​−y1​)2​, is just the Pythagorean Theorem applied to horizontal and vertical legs. The midpoint formula, M=(x1+x22,y1+y22)M=\left(\dfrac{x_1+x_2}{2},\dfrac{y_1+y_2}{2}\right)M=(2x1​+x2​​,2y1​+y2​​), averages the coordinates. The slope formula, m=y2−y1x2−x1m=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}m=x2​−x1​y2​−y1​​, measures steepness and direction, and lets you test whether two segments are parallel (equal slopes) or perpendicular (slopes multiply to −1-1−1). A horizontal segment has slope 000; a vertical segment has undefined slope. This worksheet also uses the midpoint formula in reverse to find a missing endpoint, and uses slope to test whether three points lie on the same line.

Examples

Example 1: Distance between two points
Find the distance between A(1,2)A(1,2)A(1,2) and B(4,6)B(4,6)B(4,6).
Solution
The horizontal and vertical legs are 333 and 444 — a 333-444-555 right triangle.
Identify the coordinates: (x1,y1)=(1,2)(x_1,y_1)=(1,2)(x1​,y1​)=(1,2) and (x2,y2)=(4,6)(x_2,y_2)=(4,6)(x2​,y2​)=(4,6).
Apply the distance formula:
d=(4−1)2+(6−2)2=32+42=9+16=25=5d=\sqrt{(4-1)^2+(6-2)^2}=\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=\sqrt{9+16}=\sqrt{25}=5d=(4−1)2+(6−2)2​=32+42​=9+16​=25​=5.
The points are 555 units apart.
Answer: 555.
Example 2: Midpoint of a segment
Find the midpoint of (−3,5)(-3,5)(−3,5) and (7,−1)(7,-1)(7,−1).
Solution
Identify the coordinates: (x1,y1)=(−3,5)(x_1,y_1)=(-3,5)(x1​,y1​)=(−3,5) and (x2,y2)=(7,−1)(x_2,y_2)=(7,-1)(x2​,y2​)=(7,−1).
Average the xxx-coordinates and the yyy-coordinates separately:
M=(−3+72,5+(−1)2)=(42,42)=(2,2)M=\left(\dfrac{-3+7}{2},\dfrac{5+(-1)}{2}\right)=\left(\dfrac{4}{2},\dfrac{4}{2}\right)=(2,2)M=(2−3+7​,25+(−1)​)=(24​,24​)=(2,2).
This point sits exactly halfway between the two endpoints.
Answer: (2,2)(2,2)(2,2).
Example 3: Slope through two points
Find the slope of the line through (2,−1)(2,-1)(2,−1) and (5,8)(5,8)(5,8).
Solution
Identify the coordinates: (x1,y1)=(2,−1)(x_1,y_1)=(2,-1)(x1​,y1​)=(2,−1) and (x2,y2)=(5,8)(x_2,y_2)=(5,8)(x2​,y2​)=(5,8).
Apply the slope formula:
m=8−(−1)5−2=93=3m=\dfrac{8-(-1)}{5-2}=\dfrac{9}{3}=3m=5−28−(−1)​=39​=3.
A slope of 333 means the line rises 333 units for every 111 unit it runs to the right.
Answer: 333.

yB=2(1)−(−2)=2+2=4y_B=2(1)-(-2)=2+2=4yB​=2(1)−(−2)=2+2=4.

Example 5: Testing for parallel lines
Two segments have slopes 222 and 222. Are the lines parallel?
Solution
Two distinct lines with equal slopes never meet — they are parallel.
Answer: Yes, the lines are parallel (assuming they are not the same line).
Example 6: Testing for perpendicular lines
Two segments have slopes 333 and −13-\dfrac{1}{3}−31​. Are the lines perpendicular?
Solution
Multiply the slopes: 3×(−13)=−13\times\left(-\dfrac{1}{3}\right)=-13×(−31​)=−1.
A product of −1-1−1 confirms perpendicularity.
Answer: Yes.
Example 7: Distance with negative coordinates
Find the distance between (−3,−2)(-3,-2)(−3,−2) and (5,4)(5,4)(5,4).
Solution
Identify the coordinates: (x1,y1)=(−3,−2)(x_1,y_1)=(-3,-2)(x1​,y1​)=(−3,−2) and (x2,y2)=(5,4)(x_2,y_2)=(5,4)(x2​,y2​)=(5,4).
Apply the distance formula, being careful with the double negatives:
d=(5−(−3))2+(4−(−2))2=82+62=64+36=100=10d=\sqrt{(5-(-3))^2+(4-(-2))^2}=\sqrt{8^2+6^2}=\sqrt{64+36}=\sqrt{100}=10d=(5−(−3))2+(4−(−2))2​=82+62​=64+36​=100​=10.
The two points are 101010 units apart.
Answer: 101010.
Example 8: Why a horizontal line has slope $0$
Explain why every horizontal segment has slope 000, using the points (−1,4)(-1,4)(−1,4) and (6,4)(6,4)(6,4).
Solution
m=4−46−(−1)=07=0m=\dfrac{4-4}{6-(-1)}=\dfrac{0}{7}=0m=6−(−1)4−4​=70​=0.
The yyy-coordinates never change along a horizontal line, so the numerator of the slope formula is always 000 — slope 000.
Example 9: Why a vertical line has undefined slope
Explain why every vertical segment has undefined slope, using the points (3,7)(3,7)(3,7) and (3,−2)(3,-2)(3,−2).
Solution
m=−2−73−3=−90m=\dfrac{-2-7}{3-3}=\dfrac{-9}{0}m=3−3−2−7​=0−9​.
Division by zero is undefined, so a vertical line's slope is undefined — it has no defined "steepness" because it never runs horizontally at all.
Example 10: Are three points collinear?
Determine whether A(1,1)A(1,1)A(1,1), B(3,5)B(3,5)B(3,5), and C(5,9)C(5,9)C(5,9) lie on the same line.
Solution
Slope of AB‾\overline{AB}AB: 5−13−1=42=2\dfrac{5-1}{3-1}=\dfrac{4}{2}=23−15−1​=24​=2.
Slope of BC‾\overline{BC}BC: 9−55−3=42=2\dfrac{9-5}{5-3}=\dfrac{4}{2}=25−39−5​=24​=2.
Both slopes equal 222, and the segments share point BBB, so all three points lie on the same line.
Answer: Yes, AAA, BBB, CCC are collinear.
Example 11: Application — cell towers on a city grid
Two cell towers sit at (2,3)(2,3)(2,3) and (14,11)(14,11)(14,11) on a city grid measured in kilometers. Find the distance between the towers and the coordinates where a relay station should be built exactly halfway between them.
Solution
Distance: d=(14−2)2+(11−3)2=122+82=144+64=208=413≈14.4d=\sqrt{(14-2)^2+(11-3)^2}=\sqrt{12^2+8^2}=\sqrt{144+64}=\sqrt{208}=4\sqrt{13}\approx14.4d=(14−2)2+(11−3)2​=122+82​=144+64​=208​=413​≈14.4 km.
Midpoint (relay station): M=(2+142,3+112)=(8,7)M=\left(\dfrac{2+14}{2},\dfrac{3+11}{2}\right)=(8,7)M=(22+14​,23+11​)=(8,7).
Answer: distance =413≈14.4=4\sqrt{13}\approx14.4=413​≈14.4 km; relay station at (8,7)(8,7)(8,7).
