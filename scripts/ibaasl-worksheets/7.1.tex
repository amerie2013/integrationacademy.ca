\begin{ideabox}
Use this worksheet to plan your Exploration before you start writing --- a clear plan up front saves far more time than it costs.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item Five criteria: A Presentation, B Mathematical communication, C Personal engagement, D Reflection, E Use of mathematics.
  \item A strong aim names a specific, answerable question --- not just a general area of interest.
  \item Real (self-collected or public) data strengthens both Criterion C and E far more than invented numbers.
\end{itemize}
\end{ideabox}

\sech{Worked Examples}

\begin{example}{ex0}{Example 1 --- Modelling rumour spread}
Model how news spreads through a school using geometric-growth ideas, compared against a small survey you run yourself.\soln
\textbf{Why it works:} uses Unit 1 sequences meaningfully, and pairs a model with real, self-collected data.
\end{example}

\begin{example}{ex1}{Example 2 --- Optimizing a drink can}
Use Unit 5's optimization techniques to find a cylinder's minimal-material dimensions for a fixed volume, then compare to real cans.\soln
\textbf{Why it works:} a genuine calculus application with a natural extension --- comparing theory to reality invites real reflection.
\end{example}

\begin{example}{ex2}{Example 3 --- Correlation in an unexpected pair}
Investigate whether two seemingly unrelated variables show meaningful correlation, using Unit 4's tools on a real dataset.\soln
\textbf{Why it works:} forces careful thought about correlation vs causation --- exactly what Criterion D rewards.
\end{example}

\begin{example}{ex3}{Example 4 --- Combining two strands}
Model a Ferris wheel rider's height with a sinusoidal function (Unit 3), then integrate (Unit 6) to find total vertical distance travelled.\soln
\textbf{Why it works:} combining two strands into one coherent investigation pushes Criterion E from sufficient to excellent, without needing content beyond SL.
\end{example}

\sech{Question Recap \& Answer Key}

\begin{recapbox}
\begin{multicols}{2}
\small
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
  \item Write a one-sentence aim for a topic of your own. Does it name a specific, answerable question?
  \item List the specific mathematical techniques from this course your topic will require.
  \item Outline your four main sections (introduction/aim, background, mathematical content, conclusion) in one sentence each.
  \item Will you use real data? If so, where will it come from? If not, explain why an invented scenario still serves your aim.
  \item Identify one way you could extend your basic approach to demonstrate more sophisticated mathematics.
  \item What is the biggest risk to your topic being too broad or too narrow? How will you keep it appropriately scoped?
  \item If your results don't match your model's predictions, what would you write in your reflection section?
\end{enumerate}
\end{multicols}
\end{recapbox}

\begin{keybox}
\begin{multicols}{2}
\small
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
  \item A strong aim reads like "Does X follow model Y, and how well?" --- specific enough that a stranger could tell exactly what you're investigating
  \item Answers will vary; check that every technique listed is one you've actually learned and can justify using
  \item Answers will vary; each section should have a clear, distinct purpose --- avoid overlap between background and mathematical content
  \item Real data (self-collected or public) generally strengthens Criteria C and E; an invented scenario can still work if it's used to illustrate a technique clearly and honestly labelled as illustrative
  \item E.g., comparing your model against an alternative one, or testing its reliability outside the original data range
  \item Too broad: you won't have room to go deep. Too narrow: there's nothing left to investigate. Aim for one clear question with room for a genuine "extension" step
  \item A mismatch is a strength, not a failure --- discuss why the model might fail (a missing variable, an unrealistic assumption, measurement error) to show critical reflection
\end{enumerate}
\end{multicols}
\end{keybox}
