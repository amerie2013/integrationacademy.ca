\begin{ideabox}
Correlation measures linear association; a regression line predicts, but extrapolation is risky.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item $r$ near $\pm1$: strong linear relationship; near 0: weak/none.
  \item Interpolation (inside the data range) is more trustworthy than extrapolation (outside it).
\end{itemize}
\end{ideabox}

\sech{Worked Examples}

\begin{example}{ex0}{Example 1 --- Interpret r}
A study finds $r=-0.82$ between hours of TV watched and test scores. Interpret this.\soln
Strong negative correlation --- more TV watched tends to associate with lower scores.\\[3pt]\textbf{Conclusion:} strong negative correlation.
\end{example}

\begin{example}{ex1}{Example 2 --- Interpolation}
A line $y=2.1x+8$ fits data for $x\in[0,20]$. Predict $y$ at $x=12$ and classify.\soln
$y=2.1(12)+8=25.2+8$; $x=12$ is inside $[0,20]$.\\[3pt]\textbf{Conclusion:} $y=33.2$; interpolation.
\end{example}

\begin{example}{ex2}{Example 3 --- Extrapolation risk}
Using the same line, predict $y$ at $x=80$ and explain the risk.\soln
$y=2.1(80)+8=176$; far outside $[0,20]$.\\[3pt]\textbf{Conclusion:} $y=176$ mathematically, but unreliable since it's extrapolated well beyond the data.
\end{example}

\begin{example}{ex3}{Example 4 --- Slope interpretation}
A line relating monthly rent $x$ (\$100s) to apartment size $y$ (m$^2$) is $y=3.5x+12$. Interpret the slope.\soln
Each extra \$100 in monthly rent is associated with about $3.5$ more m$^2$ of space, on average.\\[3pt]\textbf{Conclusion:} slope $3.5$: more rent associates with more space.
\end{example}

\begin{example}{ex4}{Example 5 --- Comparing correlation strength between two data sets}
Data set M has $r=0.61$ between exercise hours and mood score; data set N has $r=0.88$ between sleep hours and mood score. Which variable shows a stronger linear relationship with mood?\soln
Compare $|r|$ values: $0.88>0.61$.\\[3pt]\textbf{Conclusion:} sleep hours shows the stronger linear relationship with mood.
\end{example}

\begin{example}{ex5}{Example 6 --- Predicting and reasoning about reliability near the data's edge}
A line $y=1.8x+22$ fits data for $x\in[5,25]$. Predict $y$ at $x=24$ and comment on reliability.\soln
$y=1.8(24)+22=43.2+22=65.2$.\\[3pt]\textbf{Conclusion:} $x=24$ is inside $[5,25]$ (interpolation), so this prediction is reasonably reliable, though close to the data's edge.
\end{example}

\begin{example}{ex6}{Example 7 --- Using two data points to build a rough line, then interpreting}
A relationship passes near $(2,10)$ and $(8,34)$. Find the approximate slope and interpret it.\soln
$m=\dfrac{34-10}{8-2}=\dfrac{24}{6}=4$.\\[3pt]\textbf{Conclusion:} each unit increase in $x$ is associated with about a 4-unit increase in $y$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}{ex7}{Example 8 --- Application --- identifying a confounding variable}
A study finds a strong positive correlation between the number of firefighters sent to a fire and the amount of damage caused. Explain why this doesn't mean firefighters cause damage.\soln
Larger fires require more firefighters AND cause more damage --- fire size is the confounding variable driving both.\\[3pt]\textbf{Conclusion:} the correlation reflects a shared cause (fire size), not firefighters causing damage.
\end{example}

\begin{example}{ex8}{Example 9 --- Extrapolation with a physically impossible result}
A line $y=-5x+120$ models a runner's finishing time (minutes) vs training weeks $x$, fit for $x\in[0,15]$. Predict $y$ at $x=30$ and explain why the result signals extrapolation has failed.\soln
$y=-5(30)+120=-150+120=-30$.\\[3pt]\textbf{Conclusion:} a negative finishing time is physically impossible --- a clear sign this extrapolation, far outside $[0,15]$, has broken down.
\end{example}
