\begin{ideabox}
Correlation measures linear association; a regression line predicts, but extrapolation is risky.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item $r$ near $\pm1$: strong linear relationship; near 0: weak/none.
  \item Interpolation (inside the data range) is more trustworthy than extrapolation (outside it).
\end{itemize}
\end{ideabox}

\sech{Worked Examples}

\begin{example}{ex0}{Example 1 --- Interpret r}
A study finds $r=-0.82$ between hours of TV watched and test scores. Interpret this.\soln
Strong negative correlation --- more TV watched tends to associate with lower scores.\\[3pt]\textbf{Conclusion:} strong negative correlation.
\end{example}

\begin{example}{ex1}{Example 2 --- Interpolation}
A line $y=2.1x+8$ fits data for $x\in[0,20]$. Predict $y$ at $x=12$ and classify.\soln
$y=2.1(12)+8=25.2+8$; $x=12$ is inside $[0,20]$.\\[3pt]\textbf{Conclusion:} $y=33.2$; interpolation.
\end{example}

\begin{example}{ex2}{Example 3 --- Extrapolation risk}
Using the same line, predict $y$ at $x=80$ and explain the risk.\soln
$y=2.1(80)+8=176$; far outside $[0,20]$.\\[3pt]\textbf{Conclusion:} $y=176$ mathematically, but unreliable since it's extrapolated well beyond the data.
\end{example}

\begin{example}{ex3}{Example 4 --- Slope interpretation}
A line relating monthly rent $x$ (\$100s) to apartment size $y$ (m$^2$) is $y=3.5x+12$. Interpret the slope.\soln
Each extra \$100 in monthly rent is associated with about $3.5$ more m$^2$ of space, on average.\\[3pt]\textbf{Conclusion:} slope $3.5$: more rent associates with more space.
\end{example}
