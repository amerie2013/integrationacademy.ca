\begin{ideabox}
A radical such as $\sqrt{72}$ is an \textbf{exact} number; the calculator's $8.485\ldots$ is only an approximation. We simplify radicals so that equal numbers look equal, and we always check a rewrite on a calculator: \emph{the value must not change}. The two key rules are $\sqrt a\sqrt b=\sqrt{ab}$ and $\dfrac{\sqrt a}{\sqrt b}=\sqrt{\dfrac ab}$. There is \textbf{no} rule $\sqrt{a+b}=\sqrt a+\sqrt b$.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Simplify:} divide out the \emph{largest perfect square}: $\sqrt{72}=\sqrt{36\cdot2}=6\sqrt2$. Perfect squares: $4,9,16,25,36,49,64,81,100,121,144,\ldots$
  \item \textbf{Add/subtract} only \emph{like} radicals, after simplifying each: $3\sqrt5+7\sqrt5=10\sqrt5$, but $\sqrt2+\sqrt3$ stays as it is.
  \item \textbf{Multiply:} $(a\sqrt b)(c\sqrt d)=ac\sqrt{bd}$; use FOIL for binomials and remember $(\sqrt a)^2=a$.
  \item \textbf{Conjugates:} $(a+\sqrt b)(a-\sqrt b)=a^2-b$ is rational. To rationalise a binomial denominator multiply top and bottom by the conjugate.
  \item \textbf{Variables:} $\sqrt{x^2}=|x|$; we assume the variables are non-negative to write $\sqrt{18x^3}=3x\sqrt{2x}$.
\end{itemize}
\end{ideabox}

\begin{learnbox}
\lh{Simplifying radicals}Divide out the \textbf{largest perfect square} factor: $\sqrt{72}=6\sqrt2$. Add or subtract only \textbf{like radicals}, after simplifying each. Multiply radicands: $(a\sqrt b)(c\sqrt d)=ac\sqrt{bd}$, and $(\sqrt a)^2=a$.
\lh{Conjugates and rationalising}$(a+\sqrt b)(a-\sqrt b)=a^2-b$ is rational. To remove a radical from a denominator multiply top and bottom by $\sqrt b$ (monomial) or by the \textbf{conjugate} (binomial).
\lh{Checking}There is no sum rule: $\sqrt{a+b}\ne\sqrt a+\sqrt b$. Check any rewrite on a calculator --- equivalent exact forms have the same decimal value.
\end{learnbox}

\sech{Worked Examples}

\begin{example}{ex0}{Example 1 --- Simplify a radical}
Simplify $\sqrt{200}$.\soln The largest perfect square dividing $200$ is $100$: $\sqrt{200}=\sqrt{100\cdot2}=10\sqrt2$. \\ Check: $\sqrt{200}=14.142$ and $10\sqrt2=14.142$ \checkmark.
\end{example}

\begin{example}{ex1}{Example 2 --- Mixed and entire radicals}
(a) Simplify $\sqrt{63}$. (b) Write $7\sqrt3$ as an entire radical.\soln (a) $\sqrt{63}=\sqrt{9\cdot7}=3\sqrt7$. \\ (b) Square the coefficient and move it inside: $7\sqrt3=\sqrt{49\cdot3}=\sqrt{147}$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}{ex2}{Example 3 --- Adding and subtracting}
Simplify $5\sqrt{12}-2\sqrt{27}+\sqrt{48}$.\soln Simplify first: $\sqrt{12}=2\sqrt3$, $\sqrt{27}=3\sqrt3$, $\sqrt{48}=4\sqrt3$. \\ $10\sqrt3-6\sqrt3+4\sqrt3=8\sqrt3$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}{ex3}{Example 4 --- Multiplying}
Simplify $(3\sqrt7)(2\sqrt{14})$.\soln $6\sqrt{98}=6\cdot7\sqrt2=42\sqrt2$. \\ Check: $3(2.646)\cdot2(3.742)=59.40$ and $42(1.4142)=59.40$ \checkmark.
\end{example}

\begin{example}{ex4}{Example 5 --- Distributing}
Simplify $\sqrt3\left(\sqrt{12}+2\sqrt5\right)$.\soln $\sqrt{36}+2\sqrt{15}=6+2\sqrt{15}$. \\ The terms $6$ and $2\sqrt{15}$ are not like terms, so this is final.
\end{example}

\begin{example}{ex5}{Example 6 --- FOIL with radicals}
Expand $(2\sqrt3+1)(\sqrt3-4)$.\soln First $2\cdot3=6$; Outer $-8\sqrt3$; Inner $\sqrt3$; Last $-4$. \\ $6-4=2$ and $-8\sqrt3+\sqrt3=-7\sqrt3$: $2-7\sqrt3$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}{ex6}{Example 7 --- Squaring a binomial}
Expand $\left(\sqrt5-2\sqrt3\right)^2$.\soln $a^2-2ab+b^2$ with $a=\sqrt5$, $b=2\sqrt3$: $5-2(\sqrt5)(2\sqrt3)+4\cdot3=5-4\sqrt{15}+12$. \\ $=17-4\sqrt{15}$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}{ex7}{Example 8 --- Rationalising denominators}
Rationalise (a) $\dfrac{12}{\sqrt6}$ \quad (b) $\dfrac{4}{\sqrt7-\sqrt3}$.\soln (a) Multiply by $\dfrac{\sqrt6}{\sqrt6}$: $\dfrac{12\sqrt6}{6}=2\sqrt6$. \\ (b) Multiply by the conjugate $\dfrac{\sqrt7+\sqrt3}{\sqrt7+\sqrt3}$: the denominator is $7-3=4$, so $\dfrac{4(\sqrt7+\sqrt3)}{4}=\sqrt7+\sqrt3$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}{ex8}{Example 9 --- Radicals with variables}
Simplify $\sqrt{72a^5}$ where $a\ge0$.\soln $72=36\cdot2$ and $a^5=a^4\cdot a$, so $\sqrt{36\cdot a^4\cdot2a}=6a^2\sqrt{2a}$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}{ex0}{Example 10 --- Application: distance between points}
Find the exact distance between $A(-2,1)$ and $B(4,5)$.\soln $d=\sqrt{(4-(-2))^2+(5-1)^2}=\sqrt{36+16}=\sqrt{52}$. \\ $\sqrt{52}=\sqrt{4\cdot13}=2\sqrt{13}\approx7.21$ units.
\end{example}

\begin{example}{ex1}{Example 11 --- Test before you trust}
A student claims $\sqrt{x^2+9}=x+3$. Test it.
\begin{center}\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.35}\setlength{\tabcolsep}{3pt}\begin{tabular}{l|cccc}
$x$ & $0$ & $1$ & $2$ & $4$ \\ \hline
$\sqrt{x^2+9}$ & \makebox[1.35cm]{\strut $3$} & \makebox[1.35cm]{\strut $3.162$} & \makebox[1.35cm]{\strut $3.606$} & \makebox[1.35cm]{\strut $5$} \\
$x+3$ & \makebox[1.35cm]{\strut $3$} & \makebox[1.35cm]{\strut $4$} & \makebox[1.35cm]{\strut $5$} & \makebox[1.35cm]{\strut $7$} \\
\hline same? & \makebox[1.35cm]{\strut $\checkmark$} & \makebox[1.35cm]{\strut $\times$} & \makebox[1.35cm]{\strut $\times$} & \makebox[1.35cm]{\strut $\times$} \\
\end{tabular}\end{center}
\soln The two sides agree at $x=0$ only. One mismatch is enough to prove the claim false: there is no sum rule $\sqrt{a+b}=\sqrt a+\sqrt b$ (here $\sqrt{x^2+9}\ne\sqrt{x^2}+\sqrt9$).
\end{example}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
  \item $\sqrt{120}=\sqrt{4\cdot30}=2\sqrt{30}$.
  \item $675=225\cdot3$, so $15\sqrt3$.
  \item $4\sqrt7=\sqrt{16\cdot7}=\sqrt{112}$. Both are $10.583$.
  \item $8\sqrt2+9\sqrt2-5\sqrt2=12\sqrt2$.
  \item $15\sqrt{12}=15\cdot2\sqrt3=30\sqrt3$.
  \item $4\sqrt{50}-3\sqrt5=20\sqrt2-3\sqrt5$.
  \item $12+15\sqrt2-8\sqrt2-20=-8+7\sqrt2$.
  \item (a) $11-5=6$. \quad (b) $12+4\sqrt{21}+7=19+4\sqrt{21}$.
  \item $\dfrac{9\sqrt3}{2\cdot3}=\dfrac{3\sqrt3}{2}$.
  \item Multiply by $\dfrac{4+\sqrt{10}}{4+\sqrt{10}}$: denominator $16-10=6$; $\dfrac{6(4+\sqrt{10})}{6}=4+\sqrt{10}$.
  \item $\sqrt{49\cdot2\cdot x^4\cdot y^2\cdot y}=7x^2y\sqrt{2y}$.
  \item $\sqrt{9+16+25}=\sqrt{50}=5\sqrt2\approx7.07$ m.

  \item $(2+\sqrt5)^2=4+4\sqrt5+5=9+4\sqrt5$ and $2+\sqrt5>0$, so $\sqrt{9+4\sqrt5}=2+\sqrt5$. Calculator: $\sqrt{17.944}=4.236$ and $2+2.236=4.236$ \checkmark.
\end{enumerate}
